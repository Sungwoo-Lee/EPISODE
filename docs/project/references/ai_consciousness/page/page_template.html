Could a Machine Be Conscious?

The arguments and debates about consciousness in artificial intelligence, from many sides: what each position says, why its holders believe it, what the others answer, and what could change anyone's mind.

How to read this page

This page organises a field; it does not take a side. Every position is stated as strongly as its own holders state it, and where a source's author gives an answer, the critic's surviving counter is given too. It is built from {{N_REFS}} works: {{N_CORE}} core papers and one published debate, in which {{N_COMM}} researchers replied to the neuroscientist Anil Seth and he answered them.

The page has three parts. The first explains the words and the background. The middle, section 03, takes the field's seven questions one at a time. Each question gets its background, the positions, the arguments, how the debate moved, where it stands, and what evidence would change it. The last part uses the Seth debate as a worked example, then lists what the field agrees on, common misreadings, and what this collection leaves out. A name with a year, such as Michel 2026, links to its entry in the reference list. Click any figure to open it full size.

Numbering. Section numbers follow the source document. Its three internal working sections (7, 10 and 11) are left out, so the numbers jump from 06 to 08.
