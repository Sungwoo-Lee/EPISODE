Study summary · neuromodulator

What the Modulator Does

Eight detailed reports asked whether giving the agent a neuromodulator changes how it behaves. The short answer: its behaviour does not change, but the modulator is not idle either. This page gives the take-home messages; every claim links to the figure that backs it.

PURPOSE

The agent lives in a small grid world where it must find food, avoid predators, heal its wounds and hide in bushes. Some agents are built with a modulator: a small extra network that reads what the agent senses (including how hurt and hungry it is) and, at every step, turns the main network's internal signals up or down. The hope was that an agent with a modulator would let its body state shape what it does more than an ordinary agent does.

These reports tested that hope from several directions, over hundreds of trained agents. This page is the two-minute version. It says nothing the reports do not say; open a report for the method, the data counts and the caveats.

02 Take-home messages

Each card states a claim in plain words, its verdict, the key number, and what it means. The picture is the detailed report's own figure; click it to open it full size, or follow Detail to the figure in its report.

Not supported

Claim tested: the modulator makes behaviour depend more on the body.

With or without a modulator, agents respond to injury and hunger the same way

12 of 12comparisons where the ordinary agent sat inside the modulated agents' range

Across 64 trained agents, an injury changed hiding by the same amount with or without a modulator. Letting the modulator read only body signals made no difference, and changing the body's own rules moved both kinds of agent alike.

Supported

Claim tested: most of the modulator's output is fixed.

The modulator mostly applies the same adjustment all the time

8–15%of its output that changes with what the agent senses

The rest is a fixed turning-up or turning-down that an ordinary network could learn in its own connections. With so little that depends on the situation, there was little for a behaviour measure to find — which explains card 1.

Supported

Claim tested: the small varying part does real work.

…but the agent depends on that small varying part

−47 to −180survival steps lost, out of about 200, when it is held fixed

Hold the modulator's output at its average and the agent dies much sooner. This does not mean the modulator helps: agents trained without one do just as well. It means an agent trained with a modulator comes to rely on it.

Not supported

Claim tested: the modulated agent hides more selectively.

The differences reported earlier came from how they were measured

60 → 0percentage-point gap: last saved checkpoint alone, then averaged over recent ones

An agent's behaviour wobbles from one saved checkpoint to the next, so reading only the last one produced a large gap that averaging removes. Measured this way, both agents add the same hiding when a predator appears (about 41 points each).

Mixed

Claim tested: the modulator helps the agent survive.

A thin survival edge that does not carry across setups

about +1.6%longer survival: +3.6 steps on average across 16 worlds, +2.8 in a million-world replay

The modulated agent lived slightly longer in two studies, from single training runs. But in four matched pairs its average effect was under 0.3% of the variation in survival or hiding, and in another pair the two agents survived equally long.

Open

Claim tested: the modulator makes learning steadier.

A lead worth checking: steadier behaviour during training

2–4×steadier injury response between checkpoints, at two of five levels

In one pair of agents, the modulated agent's response to injury moved far less from one checkpoint to the next at levels 04 and 05. It is one training run each, so it needs repeating with more before it counts.

03 Still open

Nearly every result rests on one training run per design. Two runs of the same design can land in different places, so single-run differences in either direction are untested rather than settled (②, ⑥, ⑧).

A world that gives the modulator a job. In the worlds tested so far the best action rarely depends on the body, so an ordinary agent learns the same habits. A planning study looks for worlds where it does (Interactions Between Internal States), and a second plan changes the outside world between stages to see whether the modulator copes better (Continual Worlds).

The cheapest next analysis. Repeat the freezing test on modulators that read only the body. If freezing costs them nothing, the reliance in card 3 belongs to modulators that read the outside world (①, Still open).
