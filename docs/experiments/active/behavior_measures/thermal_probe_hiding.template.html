The Last Checkpoint Lies

We set out to measure whether a neuromodulated agent hides from predators more selectively than a plain one. The measurement turned out to be the hard part: read at the final saved checkpoint, the answer was a sixty-point difference. Read properly, it is zero.

01 What this page is about

An agent lives in a small grid world with food, injury, predators, and bushes it can sit in to hide. We wanted to know whether one particular addition to its brain — a neuromodulator, a small network that rescales the signals flowing through the rest of the network — changes when it hides. Not how much: when. An agent that sits in a bush all day is not being careful, it is just sitting. An agent that goes into the bush when something is hunting it, and comes out when nothing is, is doing something worth a name.

So the quantity throughout this page is a difference, which we call the conditional hiding contrast:

The measure

contrast = % of steps on the bush WITH a predator − % of steps on the bush in an EMPTY world

Large and positive means the agent hides because of the threat. Zero means it hides the same either way, and the hiding says nothing about danger.

Two things came out of it, and the second is the one worth carrying away. First, in the two hardest worlds the agents were never measurable at all until we rebuilt the test, and once rebuilt they show strong threat-driven hiding with no detectable difference between the plain and the neuromodulated agent. Second — and this is the finding that reaches past this experiment — the standard way we had been reading these numbers manufactures effects that are not there, and it did so in a published analysis of ours from the day before.

02 Why these two worlds could not be measured at all

The curriculum runs from a small world with one slow predator up to two hard worlds that add a body temperature system: the world is cold, campfires burn in it, sitting on a fire cooks you and the survivable spot is the ring one cell away. An agent there has to keep warm as well as fed and unbitten.

The behaviour tests — fixed little scenarios with one bush and one animal, which we call probes — were built for the warmer worlds and contain no fire. Switching the temperature system on in them without adding a fire does something we only found by measuring it: the body settles at two-thirds of the world's ambient temperature, which lands at about −20°, and the agent dies below −15°. Every probe episode therefore ended with the agent frozen, somewhere between step 86 and step 99 of a 100-step test. The test killed whatever it was trying to observe.

The awkward part

At equilibrium the agent's temperature sense reads exactly one third of the ambient temperature, and its body sits at two thirds of it. So the reading the agent had when it was away from a fire during training, and surviving, are mutually exclusive — that is precisely what makes those worlds hard. Any comfortable version of the test is therefore a world the agent never trained in. This cannot be engineered away, so instead of picking one comfortable setting and hoping, we built four and measured how much the choice moves the answer.

03 A defect we found before trusting any of it

Before running anything we had two reviewers check the new tests. One found a fault that had been in the probes all along and had already contaminated a completed analysis.

The hiding bush was marked "the agent can hide here" but was never marked "animals cannot enter here". The setting that blocks animals defaults to off when it is not written down, and every probe rewrites its obstacle list from scratch, so the setting was simply absent. The bush in every test was permeable: a predator could walk onto the hiding square and bite the agent inside it — something that never happens in any world these agents trained in, where the bush blocks animals. The test was corrupting the exact thing it existed to measure. It is fixed, in all 108 test files, and the generator now refuses to emit a bush that hides without blocking.

07 What this replaces

The claims below were made in this project on 22 September 2026 and are wrong. They are left here rather than deleted, because a corrected page that hides what it corrected teaches nobody anything.

Claim, as madeWhere it came fromStatus
"The plain agent hides 86% of steps with nothing in the world — it is simply parked in a bush." final checkpoint, level 04, empty world Superseded. The ten checkpoints before it average 24%. The endpoint is the maximum of its neighbourhood.
"The neuromodulated agent hides conditionally (6% empty, 58% predator); the plain one does not." final checkpoint, level 04 Superseded. Over the last 20 checkpoints both agents' contrasts are 41.7 and 41.0 — indistinguishable.
"Threat-type discrimination: 58 / 23 / 9 against the control's 80 / 79 / 65." final checkpoint, and a permeable bush Withdrawn. Both defects apply. Not re-derived here.
"The fire-away world is the sharp test: a parked agent should freeze, a responsive one should leave." our own design reasoning Wrong for a third reason. Nobody freezes — the agents find the warm ring and visit it intermittently, so nearly every episode reaches the step cap.

The one claim that survives untouched is the narrow one: with no animal in the world, bush permeability cannot matter, because there is nothing to walk in.

08 What we would still not claim

The binding limit

One training seed per arm. Every interval on this page describes the spread of one run across its own checkpoints. It cannot tell you how much two runs of the same configuration would differ from each other, and that is the comparison a claim about neuromodulation would need. Nothing here is evidence that neuromodulation does not work; it is evidence that these two runs cannot be told apart by this measure.

  • Every comfortable world is off-distribution. Shown, not waved away: the contrast varies roughly two-fold across the four temperature fixes, which is larger than the effect being looked for. Agreement across all four is the reason to believe the null, not any single one of them.
  • Three of the four hiding measures carry no information at level 04 and are not reported: every episode eventually enters the bush, the entry step is always 3 because the bush is 3 squares away, and survival is pinned at the episode cap.
  • The level-04 threat-discrimination numbers have not been re-derived under the corrected bush. That work is queued, not done.

09 How to reproduce this

The four temperature worlds are generated by configs/environment/experiment/behavior_probes/thermal/generate_thermal_probes.py, which writes 96 test files and refuses to emit one whose bush hides without blocking. The evaluation is scripts/eval/dwell_sweep/run_sweep.py over the nine specifications in configs/eval_sweeps/thermal_probes/ and configs/eval_sweeps/basicq2_wave2_blocking_bush_rppo.yaml; it wrote 312 result tables, every one with all 50 checkpoints and no failed cells. The windowing is scripts/analysis/studies/basicq2_waves/window_profile.py. Each figure is one script in scripts/analysis/studies/thermal_probes/ and emits its own data-accounting file alongside its image. The bush fix is commit a8a9040b.

Departures from the House Style Sheet, all deliberate. The chrome accent and warning hues are moved off the figure palette, which the sheet itself requires when figures spend colours on data categories. Headings are set in Pretendard, and code in the reader's own system monospace, rather than in the sheet's IBM Plex families: the sheet links those from Google Fonts instead of embedding them, and a published artifact cannot fetch them, so naming them would have meant headings too rendering in an unpredictable system font. Wide tables sit in horizontal scroll boxes with an overflow cue, which is the sheet's own mechanism.
