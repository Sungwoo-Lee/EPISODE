Body Rules and the Modulator

Sixteen versions of level 05, each with a different mix of four new body rules, trained with an ordinary agent and with a neuromodulated one. Does any mix make the modulator's behaviour depend on its body state more than the ordinary agent's does?

01 What this page asks

The modulator is a small side network that rescales the agent's brain according to its body state. It should matter most when the right action depends on combinations of body states — hurt and hungry, hurt and cold. In today's level 05 both agents learned the same simple habit, "when hurt, hide". Four body rules were built to make hunger and temperature matter more to recovery, and this experiment switched them on in every combination to see whether any combination makes the two agents' behaviour diverge. The measures, the comparisons and the bar for a real effect were written down before training; the hiding curves and Figure 1's intervals were added for this page.

The answer, in one paragraph

No mix of the four rules makes the modulator behave differently from the ordinary agent. The rules do change behaviour, sometimes a lot — with less food per bite, both agents stop hiding when hurt and hungry — but they change both agents by the same amount: none of the 15 rule effects on the difference between the agents comes near the bar set in advance. The modulator survived slightly longer (1.9–5.8 steps, about 1.6 %) in all 16 worlds, but every modulator run started from the same initial weights, so that lead is one comparison repeated, not sixteen. And the main hiding measure turned out to have an uncalibrated zero point: it reads about +0.22 even in plain level 05, so it cannot be read as "eats first when hurt and hungry".

02 Sixteen worlds, four body rules

Each world is level 05 with some of four rules switched on, at strengths chosen by short pilot runs:

  • Healing slower when hungry — healing speed falls as food energy drops below 100 and stops at 20 and below.
  • Healing uses up food — each point of injury healed costs 0.5 food energy.
  • Being cold or hot uses up food — the body burns extra food for each degree away from 0 °C.
  • Less food per bite — a bite gives 4 instead of 6, so an eating step gains +2 instead of +4.

All 16 on/off combinations were trained for 10 million episodes, once with the ordinary agent and once with the modulator agent (32 runs, one seed each). Each final agent was then recorded for 1,000,000 episodes, and everything below is measured on those recordings.

03 What is measured

Measure 1 (the main one) asks whether hiding depends on hunger. For fed agents (food energy 100 or more) and for hungry agents (under 60) separately, it takes the share of steps in cover when badly hurt (injury 60–100) minus the share when barely hurt (0–20); measure 1 is the fed value minus the hungry value. The idea was that an agent that simply "hides when hurt" would score about 0, and one that eats first when hurt and hungry would score above 0. That premise failed: on the earlier level-05 runs, with no new rules, both agents score about +0.25. Unhurt hungry agents already sit in a bush about twice as often as unhurt fed ones, which leaves less room to rise — so measure 1 is positive without any difference in what hurt agents do. It is still valid for comparing the two agents, which share the offset, but not as a measure of "eats first".

Measure 4 asks whether behaviour depends on combinations of body states: how much better the agent's activity is predicted from two body readings than from the best single one. Survival is the mean episode length over the last tenth of training. Reward is never used.

09 What this page cannot say

  • One training run per agent per world. Differences between runs of the same agent are unknown, and every run of an agent shared the same starting weights. The rule effects are judged against a noise estimate taken from the effects themselves; smaller effects than the margins cannot be seen.
  • Measure 1's zero point is not calibrated, as section 03 explains; it compares the agents fairly but does not measure "eats first when hurt".
  • Final behaviour only. Only each run's final agent was recorded; an effect that appeared mid-training and faded would be missed.
  • Level 05's world, today's sensing. The agent smells food and predators almost everywhere; a world where danger must be inferred (studied on the internal-state interactions page) might separate the agents where these rules do not.
  • What this means for the project — continue with body rules, change the world, or change the modulator — is the user's decision, recorded with the PI.
