Engineering audit · nmn_input_site_grid

Modulator Training Health

Thirty-two runs across two grids, the first to exercise a modulation refactor end to end. This asks one question: is the machinery working? Not whether the neuromodulator helps — whether it is wired, fed gradient, and training without pathology.

The grids differ in one config value, agent.return_mode: MC (Monte-Carlo returns) and GAE_NORM (generalised advantage estimation, rescaled to the same units as MC). Everything else — architecture, seed, environment, budget — is identical, and both share seed 42, so matched cells begin from bit-identical weights. Both grids complete, ten million episodes per run. Final, 2026-09-08.

Verdict

The machinery is working. No run needs stopping, restarting or discarding.

All thirty-two runs finished. Zero NaN and zero infinities in any metric, at any step, in any of them. Both controls pass their pre-registered replication gates — 164.46 against 162.4–166.9 for MC, and 170.62 against a band only 1.3 steps wide for GAE_NORM — so both grids have a valid reference point.

The two grids ran byte-identical training code: the diff between their commits over the source tree and the trainer is empty.

One finding this page previously reported as a concern has been reframed by an optimisation-theory review and is no longer one. The retraction is in place below rather than edited away.

The checks, both grids

Every run learns; nothing diverges

32/32

Survival rises from 18 to 163–171 steps (MC) and 167–173 (GAE_NORM). Losses stable; largest post-2M drawdown 20–28 steps including both controls. One JIT compile per run, no stalls, no warnings, no exact-constant series.

The modulator receives gradient everywhere

30/30

The likeliest defect in new wiring is a site nominally enabled but detached from the loss — invisible in survival numbers. Minimum gradient across all history: 0.0071 (MC) and 0.0076 (GAE_NORM), both about 7× the vanishing floor, both in the same cell. Zero points below the floor in any of the thirty modulated arms.

The two never-before-run sites, actor and critic, are confirmed live twice over: non-zero gradient and signals that travel far from initialisation, which a disconnected site could not do.

Wiring matches configuration exactly

32/32

Checked run-by-run against each run's own saved config, not by sample: every one records the right estimator, the right site block and the right sensor list. No series present that should not be; temperature and the legacy memory-gate series absent from all thirty-two; the 59 non-modulator metric keys identical across both grids.

Narrow-input arms are not degenerate

pass

The suspicion was that a 2-dimensional modulator input carries too little to learn from. The opposite: those modulators receive more gradient than the wide ones at three of four sites, and move their gains further from identity than any other arm. Both grids agree.

Neither grid approaches the clipping failure regime

pass

Mean pre-clip gradient norm 0.20–0.31 (MC) and 0.16–0.25 (GAE_NORM) against a ceiling of 0.5, with 99th percentiles beneath it in every arm. For scale, the arms that failed in this project's earlier return-estimator study sat 150–300× above the ceiling and clipped on every update.

GAE_NORM is markedly the quieter batch

pass

Six gradient excursions above 3.0 across five runs, largest 8.4 — against MC's 22 events across thirteen runs, largest 1030.

That substantially weakens the 1030 event as a defect candidate: the same fifteen architectures, same seed, same initialisation, re-run for another 160 million episodes, produced nothing above 8.4. If the modulation machinery caused it, it had every opportunity to recur.

The modulator is driven less hard under GAE_NORM

watch

Over complete histories GAE_NORM's modulator receives 0.70×–0.94× the gradient of its MC counterpart, in 15 of 15 pairs. Early in training they were at parity — MC's modulator gradient grows across training while GAE_NORM's stays flat, and the gap opens from there.

Not a health problem — every arm remains 17×–180× above the vanishing floor — but the interpretation is not the obvious one. See the optimisation reading below.

No cell-specific interaction between modulator and estimator

pass

GAE_NORM finishes above MC in 16 of 16 cells, mean +4.31 steps, spread across cells 1.59 — smaller than the 4.5-step seed-to-seed spread of the same measurement. One common estimator effect plus single-seed noise reproduces all sixteen without a configuration-specific term.

Read as health rather than outcome: the modulation machinery is not interacting with the estimator in some cell-specific way, which is what you want before treating the grids as a matched pair. Caveats: sixteen paired draws at one shared seed, not a tested hypothesis; a visible ceiling effect (correlation between a cell's MC level and its gain is −0.72); and none of it says which configuration is better.

Withdrawn: the "hot windows" difference

retracted

An earlier version of this page reported that GAE_NORM ran hotter against the clipping ceiling in the wide-input arms. Over complete runs that washes out and reverses — confined to roughly the first tenth of training; by the final tenth the control shows 71% of windows touching the ceiling under MC against 0% under GAE_NORM.

The original attribution still holds, and is why this was never a modulator finding: the effect appears in the unmodulated control at both ends, so it belongs to the estimator.

How it is computed

FiLM modulation multiplies a layer's output by a gain and adds an offset — γ and β in the code. Both are produced fresh at every timestep by the modulator reading the agent's senses, so they are a running signal, not fixed parameters. At each logging interval the trainer averages the gains emitted across that interval's whole rollout — every timestep, every parallel environment, every unit of the target layer — and records the mean. Each bar endpoint is the mean of a run's last five such intervals.

Each bar endpoint is the mean of a run's last five such intervals, which smooths sampling noise on a quantity that is itself already an average. A bar then spans the lowest and highest of those values across the six runs in that grid which enable the site, so it answers "where did this site's gain end up, across every arm that uses it" rather than "what did one run do".

Two things a bar cannot show, both of which matter. Because the value is a mean over units, a layer whose gains are uniform and one whose gains are wildly split produce the same number. And because it is also a mean over timesteps, a modulator that varies sharply with context and one that has settled on a near-constant output are likewise indistinguishable here. The second limitation is the more serious, since context-dependence is the architecture's whole premise — and it is the subject of the next section.

Provenance, and a known limitation. The ten bar endpoints are transcribed from the two end-of-training gain tables in the audit document — the MC table and the GAE_NORM table — rather than emitted by a plotting script. They were verified against that source when this page was last published. They will not update themselves if the runs are re-analysed, so anyone refreshing the audit must re-check them here. This is a declared exception to the project's rule that a figure names the script that regenerates it.

What an optimisation reading changes

The falling gains and negative offsets were reviewed against optimisation theory and the FiLM literature. Two of this page's earlier readings do not survive that; one concern replaces them, and it is sharper.

Retracted · "two offsets never converged"

This page reported that the actor and critic offsets were still sliding at the ten-million-episode budget and framed that as unfinished training. The question is ill-posed.

The time-averaged gain and offset at every site are gauge quantities: any constant pair can be folded into the modulated layer's own affine parameters — the normalisation scale and bias at the encoder, the first weight matrix and bias at the actor and critic — leaving the network's function completely unchanged. The loss is therefore flat along that direction. With no weight decay and no conservation law in Adam along a flat direction, the averages drift at learning-rate speed indefinitely.

So the drift is not a budget problem and more training would not stop it. Both grids drifting by the same 11% is consistent with a flat direction traversed at the same learning rate, not with two runs converging on the same answer.

Retracted · "gains below 1 with negative offsets look pathological"

This is the canonical FiLM outcome, not a warning sign. The original FiLM paper reports a sharp concentration of gains near zero and predominantly negative offsets. Combined with the gauge argument above, the absolute level of a mean gain carries little information on its own.

The concern that replaces them · rectifier dormancy

What the gauge does not absorb is the effective per-unit threshold each unit ends up with. A unit pushed below its rectifier's threshold in every context is in an absorbing state: it produces no output, so its own gain and offset rows receive no gradient, and it cannot recover. That is a real failure mode, it is invisible in every metric currently logged, and the pooled means in the figure above cannot distinguish it from a benign rescaling.

The planned active-unit fraction is the right instrument but needs three refinements to be decisive: the per-unit time-averaged activity as a distribution rather than a mean, so a dead unit is distinguishable from a contextually gated one; a published dormancy score rather than a home-grown threshold; and the unmodulated control's same layer as the null, since some dormancy is ordinary.

The measurement gap that matters most

Context-dependence is the entire point of this architecture, and nothing logged tests it. A modulator emitting a near-constant re-parameterisation and one varying sharply with context produce identical pooled statistics.

Two diagnostics settle it, and both are computable today from the saved checkpoints — no retraining. First, a variance decomposition of the gain table into across-unit and across-time parts, giving the fraction of variation that is genuinely contextual. Second, a freeze-at-mean counterfactual: replay a saved policy with the gains and offsets replaced by their time-averages. If behaviour is unchanged, the modulator is functionally static whatever the variance split says. The model already returns the signals; the evaluation path discards them.

Something nobody had looked at

Checking the logs to explain why GAE_NORM drives the modulator less hard ruled out the two obvious candidates — the value loss is flat across every decile in both grids, so a shrinking residual scale accounts for at most a third of the gap, and gain-compensation is unsupported.

What turned up instead: the total gradient falls over training in both grids while the modulator's does not, so the modulator's share of the squared gradient norm rises to 40–75% late in training. That is a substantive fact about what these networks end up optimising, and it was not on anyone's list.

A horizon-based explanation for the estimator difference is offered in the memo and flagged as theory, with the two logs that would test it.

And the clip is doing real work

Because clipping happens before the optimiser's moment estimates, constant clipping largely cancels but intermittent clipping does not. So the spread in how often an arm's peak touches the ceiling — from 1.7% to 91% — is a genuine difference in optimiser regime rather than a cosmetic one.

It also explains the quiet loss curves around the 1030 event: left unclipped, that single update would have inflated Adam's second-moment estimate enough to stall the affected parameters for roughly 250 iterations. Clipping is why nothing visible happened.

Three problems found, none of them in the runs

1 · The metrics reference will mislead a reader

It documents the encoder gains as pre-sigmoid and instructs applying a sigmoid. Under the mode all thirty-two runs use, those gains are linear and start at 1.0, not 2.0. Someone following it computes 0.59 for a value that is 0.375 — and the reference's own healthy band would mark all thirty-two healthy runs as sub-healthy. A documentation fix, but it wrongly condemns the runs until made.

2 · A pre-registered gate is unsatisfiable in both grids

Both studies require every run to record a clean-tree flag. MC recorded fifteen unknown and one true; GAE_NORM recorded sixteen unknown. Provenance capture shells out to git with a ten-second timeout, and sixteen processes starting within eleven seconds against a network filesystem lose that race.

Two independent demonstrations make this systematic behaviour of simultaneous multi-run launch, not bad luck. The substitute checks all pass — commit ancestry, an empty source diff, and every built model reporting the sites and sensors it should. The gate should be rewritten rather than waived case by case.

3 · "Enabling a site is a no-op at step 0" is true on average, not per unit

Only the biases of the FiLM heads are initialised to identity; their weight matrices use the default random initialisation with no zero-gate. Measured per-unit spread at the first logged point is about ±0.3 on both gain and offset in both grids — so on day one a typical unit is multiplied by roughly 0.7–1.3 and shifted by ±0.3, rather than left alone. Restate the design's argument; changing the initialisation mid-study would be worse.

What to add before the next wave

Re-ordered after the optimisation review. The first two need no new training — the signals already exist in the saved checkpoints and are discarded by the evaluation path.

  • Contextual-variation fraction, plus a freeze-at-mean replay. Now first, because it tests the architecture's premise rather than its health: does the modulator actually vary with context, or has it learned a constant re-parameterisation? Nothing currently logged can tell.
  • Per-unit activity distribution after the FiLM transform, with a published dormancy score and the unmodulated control as the null. The one genuine failure mode the gauge argument leaves standing, and an absorbing one — a unit off in every context cannot recover.
  • Per-update gradient-norm percentiles and the true clipped fraction. Lower priority than before: the completed GAE_NORM grid answered most of what this was wanted for, since the 1030-magnitude event failed to recur across another sixteen runs.
  • Parameter counts at startup. A one-line check that each arm built the network its config describes.
