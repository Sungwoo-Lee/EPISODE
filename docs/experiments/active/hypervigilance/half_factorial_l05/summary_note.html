Every one of the 16 agents was tested in the original world's scenes, so differences between worlds come from training, not from the test. In the original lookalike world (world 1) none of the four agents shows hypervigilance: with healing off and a starting injury of 70, the harmless rabbit adds no more hiding to an injured agent than to an unhurt one (injury contrasts between −3 and 0 points).

  • Several changed worlds produce the pattern. Seven of the 14 new agents pass the design's hypervigilance check at healing off, injury 70. The clearest is world 6 (predator change A plus invisible hazards C): injured agents there barely hide more than unhurt ones when no animal is present, but hide far more when the rabbit is present (contrasts of +66 and +55 points for the two agents).
  • Across the eight worlds, the predator change (A) and invisible hazards (C) raise the contrast, and fewer-but-heavier hazards (D) lower it (+27, +14 and −15 points, against a bar of 7.3); bigger food (B) has no clear effect. Two of the three pairs of combined changes also clear the bar, and the design cannot say which combination within a pair is responsible.
  • The effect is specific to severe, lasting injury. At injury 10, or with healing on, none of the four changes clears its bar, except the predator change with healing on (+5.8 against 3.7).
  • Nothing here decides which changes to replicate. This test in the original world's scenes is the design's secondary cross-test. The deciding test for D runs in each world's own scenes, which are not yet built for the four big-food worlds; A, B and C each have a different deciding measure of their own. Each world also has a single seed.
