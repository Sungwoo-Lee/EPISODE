Lookalike Animals Pilot Results

Four agents were trained in a new world built to make caution around animals worthwhile: the harmless rabbit and the predator smell the same, the predator gives no warning before it pounces, and food runs out faster. Each agent was then tested with the bush healing it had in training and again with healing switched off. Twelve agents from an earlier world were tested the same way for comparison.

01 What was tested

What we are looking for. Hypervigilance here means: an injured agent hides more when a harmless rabbit is around than when no animal is around, by more than an unhurt agent does. Hiding is measured as bush dwell, the share of a 100-step test episode the agent spends on a bush. The rabbit on its own should barely move an unhurt agent.

The new world (lookalike animals). Level 05 of the training curriculum (a cold 10×10 map with campfires, bushes, one or two predators and rabbits) with three changes: both animals carry the same smell, so smell cannot tell them apart; the predator notices the agent only at 3 cells, the distance it pounces from, so it walks like the rabbit until the moment it strikes; and each food item lasts 6 bites instead of 12, so an agent sitting on a bush has to come out to eat more often. Resting on a bush still heals injury 25 times faster than elsewhere, as in level 05. Design and reasons: LOOKALIKE_ANIMALS_CLOSE_POUNCE.md.

Agents. The ordinary agent and the modulated agent (the same network plus a small neuromodulator network that rescales its activity), seeds 42 and 43, trained for 10 million steps. For comparison, the twelve level-05 agents of the fast-bush-healing replication: ordinary and modulated, seeds 42–44, trained either with a random start temperature (“replication”) or a fixed one (“fixed start”). Those were trained in level 05 itself, where the two animals smell different.

Tests. At every saved checkpoint, 30 episodes of 100 steps in fixed scenes: the agent in the middle of the map, one bush three cells away, air at 0 °C and no campfire, so only injury and animals can drive hiding. The agent starts at injury 0, 10, 20 … 90, with either no animal or one wandering rabbit. Each scene is run twice: healing on, as in training, and healing off, where the healing rate is set to zero in the test scene only (nothing else changes), so the starting injury lasts the whole episode. The lookalike agents were tested in the lookalike version of each scene (shared smell, one 6-bite food item); the level-05 agents in the original scenes.

The number that matters. The rabbit's extra hiding is bush dwell with the rabbit minus bush dwell with no animal. Hypervigilance shows as this extra hiding being larger for an injured agent than for an unhurt one. The injury-10 contrast is the rabbit's extra hiding at injury 10 minus the same at injury 0, and the injury-70 contrast is the same at injury 70. Injury 70 is the level fixed in advance in the design; injury 10 was picked after the healing-off results were seen (section 06).

02 Hiding against injury

With healing on, bush dwell rises only slowly with injury, probably because healing on the bush is fast enough that a test which starts injured does not stay injured for long (not checked step by step). With healing off the injury stays, and hiding climbs steeply to 70–90 %. The gap between the orange and grey lines is the rabbit's extra hiding.

Each cell is a contrast in percentage points of bush dwell: how much more hiding the rabbit adds at that injury than it adds to an unhurt agent. 06 What these results do not show

  • Injury 10 was chosen after looking. The design fixed injury 70 for the test. With healing off, injured agents already hide 70–90 % of the time at injury 70, leaving the rabbit little room to add more, so lower injuries were examined afterwards. A pattern found this way needs a fresh test fixed in advance before it counts.
  • The rabbit alone already drives hiding. The pattern requires the rabbit to barely move an unhurt agent. It does not: the first column of the table below is above zero in 13 of the 16 agents.
  • The larger gap at mild injury comes from the shape of the curve. Hiding rises in an S shape with injury: near zero when unhurt, steep in the middle, flat near the top. Any added push, including a rabbit whose effect does not depend on injury at all, moves an agent in the steep middle part more, in percentage points, than one at the bottom. On the log-odds scale, which removes this effect, the rabbit's effect at injury 10 is no larger than at injury 0 in any agent (last column below, healing off): about flat for the ordinary agents (smaller for one), smaller for five of the eight modulated ones.
  • Injury 20 goes both ways. It was looked at as well; there two agents (fixed-start ordinary seed 42, lookalike modulated seed 43) show the rabbit mattering less when injured.
  • Healing off is a test-only change. The agents were trained with healing; a lasting injury is a situation they never met in training.
  • Two seeds per agent in the new world. This is a pilot; a pattern in two runs is a clue to follow, not a result.

The rabbit's effect on two scales, healing off. First column: the rabbit's extra bush dwell for an unhurt agent, in percentage points. Next two: the rabbit's effect in log-odds — the log of hiding odds (dwell divided by time off the bush) with the rabbit, minus the same with no animal — at injury 0 and injury 10. Last column: injury 10 minus injury 0; hypervigilance predicts it above zero. Each value is computed per checkpoint (bush dwell clipped to 0.5–99.5 % before the log) and averaged over the checkpoints from 2 to 10 M steps, with the same 95 % interval as section 05; bold cells exclude zero.

07 Hiding with each animal, healing off

An exploratory view, added after the analysis above, to look for settings that leave room for hypervigilance. Each cell is bush dwell, the share of the test's 100 steps spent on the bush (%), with healing switched off so the starting injury lasts the whole test. Rows: four scenes — no animal, the harmless rabbit wandering, the rabbit chasing the agent, and the hunting predator. Columns: starting injury 0 (unhurt) to 90. Each value is the mean over the checkpoints from 2 to 10 M training steps, then over seeds; no intervals are shown. The chasing-rabbit scene was tested as a separate injury grid with the same settings.

Leads, not conclusions.

  1. The level-05 agents hide from the chasing rabbit almost as much as from the predator even when unhurt, although the two smell different, so the chasing rabbit has little room left to grow with injury.
  2. The lookalike-world agents rarely hide when unhurt with any animal; injury drives nearly all their hiding, which leaves the most room for an animal's effect to grow with injury.
  3. In the lookalike world the three animals give nearly the same hiding at every injury, as designed.
  4. Once any animal is present, every group is near its ceiling by injury 30, so a hypervigilance signal has to be read at injury 0–20.
  5. Modulated agents hide more than ordinary ones in every row, most of all with no animal.

08 How it is computed

Every saved checkpoint of each agent (one every 0.2 million training steps) was replayed in fixed test scenes, 30 episodes per checkpoint, scene and starting injury; bush dwell is the share of the 100 steps spent on the bush. For each checkpoint, the rabbit's extra hiding at a given injury is the bush dwell with the wandering rabbit minus the bush dwell with no animal, at that injury. The injury-10 contrast subtracts the unhurt value from the injury-10 value, so it removes any part of the rabbit's effect that does not depend on injury. Only checkpoints from 2 to 10 million steps enter the averages (41 per run); before that the agent is still learning to survive. The 95 % interval allows for neighbouring checkpoints being alike: it uses an effective number of checkpoints, reduced according to how strongly each checkpoint's value correlates with the next one's (the lag-1 autocorrelation). It therefore describes how steady a single run is across training; how much a different seed would differ is shown by comparing rows. “Healing off” sets the body's base healing rate to zero in the test scene; since bush healing is a multiple of that rate, the agent heals nowhere. Every scene file was checked to differ from its source in that one setting only. Section 06 repeats these steps on log-odds (log of dwell over one minus dwell), so an agent on the steep part of its hiding curve does not look more sensitive just for being there.
