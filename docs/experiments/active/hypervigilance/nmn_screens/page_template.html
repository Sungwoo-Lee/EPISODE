01 Settings and tests

The modulator. The modulated agent has a small extra network that reads the agent's senses and, at each of four places in the main network, multiplies every neuron's activity by a learned gain and adds a learned offset. The current version has a memory of 16 units, gives each neuron its own gain (grouping 1) and reads all 58 sensed numbers. The fast-bush-healing replication found that this version does not reliably make injury-driven hiding larger than in the ordinary agent.

Capacity screen (nine settings). Modulator memory 32, 64 or 128 units, crossed with 8, 16 or 32 neurons sharing one gain and offset (16, 8 or 4 shared gains per 128-neuron layer). Everything else is the current modulated agent.

Input screen (four settings). Current memory and grouping; the modulator reads only: felt injury (N, 1 number); fullness and felt injury (I, 2); all body signals — fullness, body temperature, felt injury (IT, 3); or the outside world only — contact, heat, smell, collision, vision (X, 49). In every setting the main network still reads all 58 numbers.

Training and tests. Level 05 (cold air, campfires, fast healing on bushes, random start temperature), 10 million steps, seed 42 only. Tests as in the replication: at every saved checkpoint, 30 episodes of 100 steps in fixed scenes — the agent in the middle of a 10×10 map, one bush three cells away, no animal, a harmless wandering rabbit, a chasing rabbit or a hunting predator — starting unhurt (injury 0) or injured (70), and at injuries 0–90 for the dose figures. Neutral scenes (air 0 °C, no campfire) and own scenes (training-like cold air, campfire beside the bush). Bush dwell is the share of the 100 steps spent on the bush. References: the replication's level-05 ordinary and current modulated agents, seeds 42–44.

03 Stage-2 decision table

Every setting's effects in percentage points (pp) of bush dwell, beside the range of the six references. The two rule columns are for reference only — the choice of which settings get seeds 43 and 44 is made by judgement. Strict: injury effect with the rabbit at least 12.2 pp and with no animal at least 14.7 pp, each interval's lower end above the six-reference maximum (neutral scenes). Option A: rabbit effect at least the reference maximum (9.2 pp), no-animal effect at least the reference median (10.65 pp), and own-scene effects not below the weakest reference. With one seed, the strict rule passes a true +3 pp improvement about 10 % of the time and option A about 47 %; option A also passes a setting with no real effect about 17 % of the time.

06 How it is computed

Each agent's saved checkpoints were replayed in fixed test scenes, 30 episodes per checkpoint, scene and starting injury, and bush dwell recorded as the share of the 100 steps spent on the bush. For each checkpoint three differences are formed: injured (starting injury 70) minus unhurt (0) with no animal; the same with the wandering rabbit; and the hunting predator minus no animal, both unhurt. Only checkpoints from 2 to 10 million training steps are used, one every 0.2 million steps (41 per run), and each difference is averaged over them. The 95 % interval allows for neighbouring checkpoints being alike: it uses an effective number of checkpoints, reduced by the correlation between each checkpoint and the next (the lag-1 autocorrelation). It describes how steady one run is across training, not how much a second seed would differ; the six references show that seed-to-seed spread. In the training figures each line averages 5 neighbouring checkpoints only to make ten overlapping lines readable. Every new setting has one seed, so a setting that stands out here is a candidate for more seeds, not a result.
