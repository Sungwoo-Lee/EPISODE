Interactions Between Internal States

A planning study before any training. Using the body's own rules and an ideal planner, it asks which level-05 settings make the best action depend on several body states at once — food energy, injury and body temperature together — and which do not.

01 What this page asks

Our trained agents learned a simple habit: when injured, hide and rest. That habit is right whatever the agent's hunger or temperature, so an ordinary network learns it as well as a neuromodulated one. A modulator — a side network that rescales the agent's brain according to its body state — should help when the best action depends on combinations of body states: injured and hungry, injured and cold.

Five new body rules are being built (B1–B5), and three existing settings could be changed (A1, A4, A5). Before spending training time, this page asks: for which settings does the best action depend on more than one body state at once? It does not train anything. It copies the body's rules into a small simulator, reduces level 05 to five places, and computes the best choice in every body state with an ideal planner. Everything here describes what an ideal agent would do — a guide for choosing worlds, not evidence about trained agents.

The answer, in one paragraph

The biggest thing keeping today's level 05 simple is a bush on a fire ring: in the 41 % of episodes that have one, "go and rest in the warm bush" is best almost everywhere, and the best choice depends much less on combinations of body states. Of 25 single changes, one clears the pooled rule fixed before the data — making cover scarce (a bush about 8 steps away), by about 7 points — but 5 of those 7 points come from the episodes with a warm bush, where the change mostly breaks that warm bush's dominance; in the other 59 % its gain (+3) is inside the noise. Healing that needs food (B5) is the reverse: +5.4 in episodes without a warm bush, above the line, and nothing with one. Healing that costs food (B3), warmth that costs food (A1) and injury that speeds cooling (B4) stay within the noise; scarcer food (A4) and healing that needs warmth (B2) make the best choice simpler.

02 How it works

  1. The body, copied exactly. A small program repeats the environment's body update line by line: food energy (0–200, ideal 100; −1 per step; +5 per bite; death at 0 and 200), injury (healing only while resting, 0.2 per step in the open, 5 in a bush; death at 100), body temperature (death outside −15 to +15), and the environment's reward — how much closer the body gets to its ideal, −100 on death. It was checked against the real environment on 4,000 random body states, including states near starvation, over-eating, freezing and death by injury: the largest difference was 0.00002, and death was decided identically every time.
  2. The world, reduced to five places, with every number measured from 300 real level-05 starts and the level-05 agents' training recordings: open cold ground (−30°), a bush in the cold, a bush on a fire ring (+8.8°, present in 41 % of episodes), the open fire ring, and a food item. Each is a median of 2 steps away. Outside a bush, predators add injury on average — 0.12 per step while resting, 0.70 while moving — and nothing inside one.
  3. The ideal planner. For every combination of food energy, injury, body temperature and place, it computes the choice that gives the best long-run reward, looking ahead the way our agents do (a discount of 0.95, read from their training configs; 0.99 is checked too). The choices are grouped into four: rest in cover, warm up, eat, stay in the open.
Combination gain
How much better the best choice can be predicted when two body states are known instead of the most informative single one — in points of accuracy, over the states a training episode can start in. Zero means one body state always settles the choice; higher means the choice needs combinations. This is the measure the rule uses.
Need balance
The share of starting states in which each choice is best. A world where one choice wins almost everywhere teaches a single habit.
Tie
A state where the best two choices are worth almost the same (within 0.5 reward units). Ties are left out of the accuracies, because the planner's preference there is noise.
Noise floor
How much the combination gain moves when today's world is re-solved on a finer grid: 1.2 points. Differences smaller than twice this are not read. Measured per map, it is 1.6 points without a warm bush and 0.5 with one.

The rule was written down before any result was read (study plan, Revisions 1 and 1b): a world is recommended when its combination gain is at least 5 points above today's and above twice the noise floor, no choice wins in more than 80 % of starting states while another than "rest in cover" wins in at least 10 %, and the ideal agent survives at most 5 points less often than today.

07 What this suggests for the level-05 variants

The test batch planned earlier trains one change at a time: A1 (staying warm costs food), B3 (healing costs food), A4 (scarcer food) and A5 (fewer bushes). Read map by map, the planner points to a different priority:

  1. Keep bushes off the fire rings — not in the batch, and perhaps the cheapest lever. Today's world without a warm bush already has a combination gain of 18.1 against 13.6 pooled; a bush on a fire ring makes "rest in the warm bush" the answer to almost everything. No setting controls this today (food has a minimum distance from fires; bushes do not), so it would need a small placement rule.
  2. B5, healing needs food — the strongest change where there is no warm bush (+5.2 to +5.4 at floors 0 and 0.2; +1.6 at 0.5; above the line and above that map's noise floor of 3.2) and should be in the batch on that evidence. It does nothing on the warm-bush map, and at the longer horizon, where 43 % of states are ties, it falls below today's value.
  3. A5, fewer bushes — passes the pooled rule, mostly by removing the warm-bush shortcut. Most of its gain is on the warm-bush map (+6.7 to +12.5); on the other map it is within the noise (−0.9 to +3.0). The passing trip (8 steps) is harsher than one randomly placed bush (typically 6 steps away on this map); two or three bushes give a trip of about 4–5 steps (uniform-placement estimate, bush_trip_estimate.json). If the placement rule in item 1 is added, A5 may add little.
  4. B3, healing costs food — small: within the noise without a warm bush (−0.9 to +0.7), and up to +3.0 with one at the highest cost (2 food per point healed).
  5. A1, staying warm costs food — no effect (−1.5 to −0.1): the ideal agent already avoids the cold, so the extra food cost rarely matters.
  6. A4, scarcer food — makes the choice simpler on both maps (−0.6 to −4.7), in every version and at every margin: hunger takes over more states and the other needs matter less — the "one need dominates" risk raised when this batch was planned.
  7. B2, healing needs warmth, also makes the choice simpler without a warm bush (−3.4 to −6.0): warming up and healing then point the same way.

Worlds combining these (for example no warm bush with B5, or with B3) were not solved; the plan tested one change at a time. That is the natural next sweep before choosing the final configurations.

08 What this page cannot say

The binding limit

This is an ideal planner, not a trained agent. It knows its injury exactly (the agent feels it through a delayed signal), plans perfectly, and lives in a reduced five-place world. A world where the ideal choice depends on combinations is one where a modulator could help; whether a trained agent uses that is what the training runs will test.

  • The new rules match their implementation. B1–B5 were written from their plan (Revision 3) before the code existed, then checked against it once it landed (commit 79cf90ef) at mid values, the sweep's extremes (B3 cost 2, B2 1/10, B4 gain 2, B5 floor 0) and the alternative modes: largest difference 0.00002, no disagreement on any death. Today's rules are already checked (4,000 states, largest difference 0.00002).
  • Predators are an average cost. The planner sees 0.12 or 0.70 injury per step outside cover, not single attacks of about 21; it under-counts sudden deaths at high injury, and agents choose where they rest, so the resting rate partly reflects that choice.
  • Food never runs out in the planner (a real item allows 12 bites), so eating looks cheaper than it is.
  • Only starting states are scored, at open ground; the modulator would matter over every state an agent visits.
