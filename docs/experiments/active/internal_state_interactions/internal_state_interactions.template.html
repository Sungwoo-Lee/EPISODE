Interactions Between Internal States

A planning study before training. Using the body's own rules and an ideal planner, it asks which level-05 settings give a balanced world — where hiding, eating and warming each matter and the best action depends on food energy, injury and body temperature together — and what the coming training runs should measure to find one.

01 What this page asks

Our trained agents learned a simple habit: when injured, hide and rest. That habit is right whatever the agent's hunger or temperature, so an ordinary network learns it as well as a neuromodulated one. A modulator — a side network that rescales the agent's brain according to its body state — should help when the best action depends on combinations of body states: injured and hungry, injured and cold.

New body rules were built to make hunger and temperature matter more, so that recovering from injury is no longer the only thing that counts. But pushed too far, the agent would simply forage or warm up all the time, hiding would collapse, and the world would be one-sided again in the other direction. What is wanted is a balanced world. This page asks where that balance lies, using an ideal planner on a simplified copy of level 05 — and turns the answer into what the training runs, with ordinary agents only, should measure. The planner is a guide for where to look; trained agents decide.

Sections 08–10 then ask a related question: whether the agents adapt to danger they cannot sense, and whether a larger world with a shorter smell range can make searching for food a real task without breaking the balance.

The answer, in one paragraph

In today's level 05, the ideal agent's time is already balanced: it hides, warms up and eats in a good share of steps, and each need drives its own behaviour (hungry agents eat 3.6 times as often as fed ones, injured agents hide 8.3 times as often). That holds in 21 of the 29 worlds tested (Figures 1 and 2); the other 8 all change predator pressure — with predators halved, hiding becomes too rare, and with predators doubled, hunger and injury stop driving their own behaviour — so balance has a window along predator pressure, not along food pressure. Two things are not balanced and no setting fixes them in the planner. Deaths are one-sided: once predators strike as single large hits — some lethal — injury causes 94–100 % of deaths after the first 20 steps, in every world. And no setting raises how much the best choice depends on combinations of body states past the bar of 9.3 points: at the pre-registered reading no change adds more than +0.7, and at a longer planning horizon the best is +5.0 (all four rules of the training experiment); stronger predators lower it by about 5.5, because hiding then wins everywhere. Real ordinary agents in the pilot runs differ from the planner in one useful way: they also starve (38 % of deaths in plain level 05), and the food-draining rules shift deaths from predators toward hunger — something only training can measure.

Two corrections to earlier versions

26 September: the first version put food a median of 2 steps away, because the measurement counted the hiding predators' places as food; food is 4 steps away, and the single-rule results (second half of this page) are the re-run. 27 September: a question arose whether the eating cost was left out — it was not (the simulator nets +5 per bite, then −1 per step, so an eating step gains +4; checked against the real environment). The "less food per bite" setting of the training experiment (bite 4) therefore halves the gain per eating step, from +4 to +2.

02 Everything in the environment that can tip the balance

Before choosing what to vary, every setting that can change how costly, urgent or dangerous eating, hiding, warming or moving is was listed — 109 of them, with their level-05 values and whether the planner models them (docs/experiments/active/internal_state_interactions/BALANCE_SETTINGS_INVENTORY.md). The ones that matter most and were not in the planner's first version:

  • Predator hits are single, sometimes lethal events. A predator hit does 15 to 120 injury; in the level-05 recordings 4 % of all hits (and 10 % of hits of 15 or more) kill an unhurt agent outright. The planner used to see only an average cost per step.
  • Food runs out. An item allows 12 bites, then reappears elsewhere, so the agent must search again. The planner's food never ran out.
  • Injury is felt late. The agent does not see its injury, only a smoothed, delayed signal; a starting injury is not felt at all on the first step. The planner knows its injury exactly — the main limit of this page.
  • No place holds body temperature at 0. The warm ring settles the body at +5.9°, open ground at a lethal −20°; staying near 0 means moving back and forth. The fire cell itself kills by heat in two or three steps.
  • Smaller ones: a hit lands over three steps and blocks healing meanwhile; the agent can start anywhere, even on a fire; fire, rock and bush look identical; moving costs no extra food.

03 What changed in the planner, and how balance is judged

Two of those were added, each checked before use: food runs out (after each bite, a 1-in-12 chance the item moves and the trip must be paid again; the rollouts show 0.083 moves per bite, as intended), and predator hits are drawn per activity from the level-05 training recordings — outside cover, a hit comes on 5.6 % of eating steps, 2.7 % of moving steps and 0.2 % of resting steps, and 2 %, 5 % and 19 % of those hits are lethal. With both switched off, the new planner reproduces the earlier results exactly.

A world counts as balanced if it meets five criteria written down before any result was read (study plan, Revisions 2–2b), on the map without a warm bush — the common one, and the only one once bushes are kept away from fires:

  1. Time. No activity takes more than 70 % of steps; hiding, warming and eating each take at least 10 %.
  2. Each need drives its own behaviour. Hungry agents eat at least twice as often as fed ones; badly injured agents hide at least twice as often as barely injured ones; "warm up" is the best choice at least twice as often when cold as when warm.
  3. Deaths. No single cause accounts for more than 60 % of deaths after the first 20 steps.
  4. Survival. At least 80 % of today's.
  5. Injury still drives hiding among well-fed agents (at least twice as often).

A sixth asks whether the best choice depends on combinations more than today, by a margin that survives how "equally good" choices are counted (9.3 points on this map).

04 Which worlds are balanced?

05 Does balance peak at a middle food pressure?

Two checks that do not change the verdict. Planning further ahead (discount 0.99 instead of the agents' 0.95) raises some worlds more: all four rules of the training experiment +5.0, healing slower when hungry +4.6, a weaker bush +4.2 — still under the bar. The bar itself is high because single predator hits make many choices nearly equally good: today's gain is 16.2, 18.5 or 9.1 points depending on how close two choices must be to count as a tie, and the bar takes that spread. With the widest tie margin, two halved-predator worlds with plenty of food (8 and 10 per bite) would pass (+11.2, +10.9). And the warming criterion is nearly empty for an ideal agent: it almost never gets cold, so "warm up is best when cold, not when warm" passes by default, and the same ratio measured over time (share of steps on a warm cell when cold vs warm) is only 1.14 today.

07 What the training runs should measure

The planner says time and needs are balanced but cannot see mistakes, felt injury or real predators. The balance search should therefore train ordinary agents only across settings, and judge each world with the same criteria, measured on the agents themselves:

  1. Time split — share of steps hiding in a bush, on a warm cell, eating, elsewhere. Can be counted during training.
  2. Each need drives its own behaviour — eating when hungry vs fed, hiding when badly vs barely injured. Countable during training from the body state and the action at each step; injury measured both as true injury and as the felt signal. Being on a warm cell when cold vs warm is logged but, decided now, not a pass/fail criterion: an agent that keeps its temperature steady is rarely cold, so the ratio is near 1 even for the ideal agent (1.14 today).
  3. What it dies of — already logged.
  4. Survival steps — already logged.
  5. Combinations — the hiding gap among hungry vs fed agents during training; the full combination measure (does knowing two body states predict the choice better than one) from recorded episodes after training.

The thresholds of section 03 carry over as a starting point, except warming as above; deaths, which the planner cannot judge, become the most informative criterion in training. The planner also says where to sweep: predator pressure, where balance has a window, alongside food pressure. The implementation — which counters to add to training logging, their cost, and tests — is the next plan.

Hidden context and exploration

A modulator should help most when the right behaviour depends on something the agent cannot sense and must infer — how dangerous this episode is, where food might be. In today's level 05 almost nothing is hidden: smells carry 20 cells on a 10 × 10 map. The three sections below ask whether today's agents adapt to the one hidden danger they have, whether knowing the danger is worth anything, and whether larger worlds with a shorter smell range can make searching a real task while staying balanced (study plan: docs/experiments/active/context_exploration/STUDY_PLAN.md).

10 Can a larger world make searching a real task?

What this means. Each search for food costs the agent twice: it burns food energy while walking, and it walks in the open where predators strike. Richer food items did not visibly help in these worlds, and they cannot help much in principle, because eating past 200 kills, so one find cannot be banked. A proposal, not yet tested: searching might become a real task without starving the agent if finding food again gets easier — food that regrows in the same place, so an agent that remembers where it ate can return. That needs a new environment rule, and it is the kind of memory a modulator might help with. Until then, the worlds that are balanced and still make the agent search a little (about 9–12 steps: for example a 10 × 10 grid with a 5-cell smell range and 1–2 rich items, or 20 × 20 with today's food count) are the natural first training candidates — and a short smell range also hides predators, turning the danger of Figure 4 into something the agent has to infer.

11 How it works

  1. The body, copied exactly. A small program repeats the environment's body update line by line: food energy (0–200, ideal 100; −1 per step; +5 per bite; death at 0 and 200), injury (healing only while resting, 0.2 per step in the open, 5 in a bush; death at 100), body temperature (death outside −15 to +15), and the environment's reward — how much closer the body gets to its ideal, −100 on death. It was checked against the real environment on 4,000 random body states, including states near starvation, over-eating, freezing and death by injury: the largest difference was 0.00002, and death was decided identically every time.
  2. The world, reduced to five places, with every number measured from 300 real level-05 starts and the level-05 agents' training recordings: open cold ground (−30°), a bush in the cold, a bush on a fire ring (+8.8°, present in 41 % of episodes), the open fire ring, and a food item. The bushes and the fire ring are a median of 2 steps away; food is 4 (corrected — see the box above). Outside a bush, predators add injury on average — 0.12 per step while resting, 0.70 while moving — and nothing inside one.
  3. The ideal planner. For every combination of food energy, injury, body temperature and place, it computes the choice that gives the best long-run reward, looking ahead the way our agents do (a discount of 0.95, read from their training configs; 0.99 is checked too). The choices are grouped into four: rest in cover, warm up, eat, stay in the open.
Combination gain
How much better the best choice can be predicted when two body states are known instead of the most informative single one — in points of accuracy, over the states a training episode can start in. Zero means one body state always settles the choice; higher means the choice needs combinations. This is the measure the rule uses.
Need balance
The share of starting states in which each choice is best. A world where one choice wins almost everywhere teaches a single habit.
Tie
A state where the best two choices are worth almost the same (within 0.5 reward units). Ties are left out of the accuracies, because the planner's preference there is noise.
Noise floor
How much the combination gain moves when today's world is re-solved on a finer grid: 1.0 point. Differences smaller than twice this are not read. Measured per map, it is 1.6 points without a warm bush and 0.2 with one.

The rule was written down before any result was read (study plan, Revisions 1 and 1b): a world is recommended when its combination gain is at least 5 points above today's and above twice the noise floor, no choice wins in more than 80 % of starting states while another than "rest in cover" wins in at least 10 %, and the ideal agent survives at most 5 points less often than today.

16 What this suggests for the level-05 variants

Since the first version of this page, the training plan changed: every combination of four rules — healing that needs food (B5), healing that costs food (B3), warmth that costs food (A1) and scarcer food (A4) — is being trained, sixteen worlds in all, and a rule keeping bushes off fire rings is being built. Read with the corrected food distance, the planner says:

  1. Keeping bushes off the fire rings is still the largest lever. Today's world without a warm bush has a combination gain of 17.1 against 4.3 with one. That rule is now being built (two cells of clearance around each burning fire).
  2. B3, healing costs food — the largest of the four rules in the training batch, but fragile (+3.1 and +3.2 pooled at 1 and 2 food per point; +0.4 and −0.3 at 0.25 and 0.5). It is inside the noise band on the common map, and it disappears at the wider tie margin and the longer horizon. Figure 8 shows what it changes: how fed the agent is decides between warming up and hiding.
  3. B5, healing needs food — small (+1.6 pooled at floors 0 and 0.2, all from episodes with a warm bush; +0.1 at 0.5). The first version of this page ranked it first; that came from the wrong food distance.
  4. A5, fewer bushes — below the rule (+1.8 to +3.7 pooled), almost all of it on the warm-bush map. Figure 7 pools its maps at today's 41 % warm-bush share, but a sparse world has fewer warm bushes: about 8 % of episodes with one bush, since 7.8 % of bushes sit on a fire ring in real resets. Reweighted to that share, trips of 6 and 8 steps score 15.1–16.5 — about what today's world itself scores at the same share (16.1). So fewer bushes work by removing warm bushes, the same lever as item 1, not through the longer trip. (A trip of 8 is longer than even a single randomly placed bush gives on this map — a median of 6 — so it is an upper bound, not a world the map produces.)
  5. A1, warmth costs food — no effect (−0.9 to −0.1): the ideal agent already avoids the cold.
  6. A4, scarcer food — within the noise at the rule's reading (−0.7 to −1.3 pooled), lower at the wider margin (down to −5.0) and the longer horizon, where the margin's noise was not measured: hunger takes over more states.
  7. B2 makes the choice simpler (down to −4.9 pooled); B4 is within the noise (down to −1.3).

So the planner no longer singles out any rule in the training batch; the sixteen-world training run is a screen, not a confirmation. One more reading follows from the two maps: B3's pooled gain is carried by the episodes with a warm bush (+3.4 and +6.2); in episodes without one — the only kind a world with bushes kept off fire rings has — no rule in the batch moves the gain beyond the noise (B3 +2.9 and +1.1, B5 +0.1, A1 and A4 slightly negative). The sixteen worlds keep today's bush placement, so both kinds of episode are in them. Worlds combining two or more rules were not solved here.

17 What this page cannot say

The binding limit

This is an ideal planner, not a trained agent. It knows its injury exactly (the agent feels it through a delayed signal), plans perfectly, and lives in a reduced five-place world. A world where the ideal choice depends on combinations is one where a modulator could help; whether a trained agent uses that is what the training runs will test.

  • The new rules match their implementation. B1–B5 were written from their plan (Revision 3) before the code existed, then checked against it once it landed (commit 79cf90ef) at mid values, the sweep's extremes (B3 cost 2, B2 1/10, B4 gain 2, B5 floor 0) and the alternative modes: largest difference 0.00002, no disagreement on any death. Today's rules are already checked (4,000 states, largest difference 0.00002).
  • Predators are an average cost. The planner sees 0.12 or 0.70 injury per step outside cover, not single attacks of about 21; it under-counts sudden deaths at high injury, and agents choose where they rest, so the resting rate partly reflects that choice.
  • Trip lengths are medians. Food is 4 steps away and the bushes and fire ring 2, as medians over real starts; the spread (food: 90 % within 8 steps) is not modelled. The first version of this page used a food trip of 2 because the measurement counted the hiding predators' places as food.
  • Food never runs out in the planner (a real item allows 12 bites), so eating looks cheaper than it is.
  • Only starting states are scored, at open ground; the modulator would matter over every state an agent visits.
