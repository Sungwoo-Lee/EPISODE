The Body's Arithmetic

How this agent's reward is computed from the state of its own body — and what changed on 22 September 2026, when being too full became as dangerous as being too empty.

01 What this page is about

The agent in this project is not scored for collecting things. It is scored for keeping its own body near a target. Three quantities describe that body: how full it is, how injured it is, and — in the one world that has a temperature system — how warm it is. Those three are combined into a single number called the homeostatic drive: the distance between where the body is and where it wants to be. Reward is then just the drop in that distance from one step to the next.

The whole rule

reward at this step = drive before the step − drive after the step

So nothing in the code ever says "eating is good". Eating is good when the agent is hungry and bad when it is full, and both fall out of that one subtraction.

Until 22 September 2026 that was only half true, because fullness could not be too high. Its target sat at the very top of its range, so more food was always better and the only way to die of hunger was to run out. This page documents the arithmetic as it now stands, with the change and its consequences stated where they arise. Every number and every curve here is read from the live environment code, not re-derived from these formulas — a page describing how reward is computed is worth nothing if it describes what its author believed.

The one thing to take away

The body now has a target in the middle of its range rather than at the end of it. That turns feeding from something to maximise into something to regulate, which is the entire reason for the change — internal state only drives behaviour if getting it wrong in either direction costs something.

04 Do hunger, injury and cold weigh the same?

The drive adds three unlike things: fullness points, injury points, and degrees. For that sum to mean anything, being equally close to death on any one of them has to count equally. This is the check the September change could most easily have broken, so it is worth doing rather than assuming.

06 What the agent actually receives

The drive is computed in raw body units, but the agent never sees those. Each body channel is divided by its own ceiling before it reaches the network, so everything arrives between 0 and 1. That rescaling is where the change does something that is easy to miss.

A correction worth recording

Death by over-eating had been a configurable option in this environment since June. It did not work. The setting wrote a "died of over-eating" label onto the step and never actually ended the episode — and because the label was keyed to a quantity that sits at its ceiling whenever the agent is merely full, that label appeared routinely on ordinary steps where nothing had happened. Three separate comments in the source described the feature as working.

Both halves are now fixed, and fixed in a way that cannot come apart again: the label and the ending read the same condition, so a label without a death is no longer possible. Measured afterwards, the label appeared on zero non-terminal steps across 1,630 steps of play.

08 Settled, and still open

SettledStill open
Fullness runs 0 to 200 with its target at 100, and both ends are lethal. The temperature axis still uses its ceiling as its scale. That equals the correct deviation range only because its target sits at zero in a symmetric band — the same assumption just removed from fullness. Harmless at every shipped value; it would need revisiting if a world ever moved the temperature target.
Every axis sits exactly 100 drive units from its own death, equal to the death penalty. Being paid to rest while overfed is correct but untested in training. Whether it produces a visible “sit still and digest” behaviour is an empirical question nobody has asked yet.
The temperature conversion is unchanged in value (6.67) and now correct in derivation. One analysis figure that counts how episodes end recognises only three outcomes, so over-eating deaths will silently score zero and its totals will not add up. Recorded, not yet fixed.
Over-eating genuinely ends the episode, with the label and the ending sharing one condition. A fullness-to-satiation power other than 1 would put the target off-centre in nutrition while keeping it centred in satiation. Nothing ships that way and nothing checks it.
Degenerate configurations — a zero-width fullness axis, a target outside its own range — are rejected when the configuration loads rather than producing a silent non-number during training.
