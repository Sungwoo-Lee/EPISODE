Hide or Forage Balance

In eight candidate training worlds, does an agent ever face a real choice between staying safe in a bush and going out to eat, or does one option always win? An agent-free answer from an ideal planner, motivated by what kills today's trained agents.

PURPOSE

Trained agents in the current level-05 world die mostly from predators, and many of those deaths happen while they are out finding food. A planned experiment changes four groups of world settings — the predator, the food, whether traps and rocks can be seen, and how many hazards there are — in eight combinations. Before training agents in those worlds, this page asks a narrower question with no agent at all: in each world, is hiding in a bush sometimes the better move and going out to eat sometimes the better move, depending on how hungry and injured the agent is? If one option always wins, the world cannot teach an agent to weigh the two.

Scope. The planner asks whether body state alone — food energy, injury, body temperature — ever makes eating and hiding comparably good. Predators, traps and rocks enter only as an average chance of being hit on each step spent outside a bush. There is no predator position, no cue, no lookalike animal. So this page cannot say whether these worlds create a dilemma when a predator is near; that needs trained agents.

The answer, in brief

__SUMMARY__

01 Words used on this page

Ideal planner
A program that, for every combination of food energy, injury, body temperature and place, works out the choice with the best long-run reward under the world's rules. It knows its body state exactly and never makes a mistake, so it shows what the rules reward, not what a trained agent does. It maximises the environment's own reward (the decrease in the body's distance from its comfortable state, minus 100 on death) with discount 0.95, the training runs' own setting.
Value iteration
How the planner is solved: every state's value is repeatedly replaced by the best one-step reward plus the discounted value of where that choice leads, until the values stop changing.
Discount
How much a reward one step later counts compared with one now (0.95: about a 20-step horizon).
Start states
The body states a training episode can begin in: food energy 0–200, injury 0–100, body temperature −10 to +5°, the agent standing on open ground. The planner's grid has {{n_starts}} of them; shares on this page are shares of these.
Hide / eat / other
The planner's best next move, grouped: hide = go to or stay in a bush; eat = go to food or eat; other = warm up at a fire or stay in the open.
Near-tie
A start state where the best "hide" move and the best "eat" move are worth almost the same (within 1 reward unit for the near-tie share; within 0.5 when "ties dropped" readings leave such states out).
Hungry start states
Start states with food energy below 100, the setpoint the reward pulls toward ({{hungry_frac}} % of all start states). Above 100, eating only moves the body away from its setpoint, so it is never best.
Half factorial
A design that tests four two-level groups in 8 of the 16 possible combinations, chosen so each group's effect can still be estimated: the average of the four worlds where it is new minus the four where it is not (its main effect). The price is that each main effect is mixed with a three-way interaction of the other groups.
Bush healing
Resting in a bush heals 25 times faster than resting in the open (5 injury points per rest step). The user's rule fixes this in training; it is changed on this page only inside the planner, for understanding.

02 Motivation: what kills trained agents

These figures read already-aggregated counts from 27 million recorded evaluation episodes (2026-10-11): the Wave-2 agents of levels 02–06, one ordinary and one modulated agent per level, and, for level 05, also the agents of the single-channel-smell study. The Wave-2 "level 06" was recorded on 2026-09-22, before the levels were renumbered on 2026-09-30; that world is today's level 07 (sensory noise) and is labelled so. At level 05 the ordinary agent survives {{a_surv}} % of episodes, starves in {{a_starv}} % and dies of injury in {{a_inj}} % (Figure 1); predators cause {{a_pred_of_inj}} % of those injury deaths.

Where are agents when the fatal hit comes? At level 05, {{a_ate_share}} % of predator deaths fall in the twenty steps after eating, but that is mainly because agents spend {{a_ate_steps}} % of their steps there. Per step, heading to food ({{a_rate_tow}} deaths per 1,000 steps) and roaming ({{a_rate_oth}}) are about four times as dangerous as the period just after eating ({{a_rate_ate}}), and no store has a single death in a bush (Figure 2).

Agents are also hungrier than usual when the fatal hit comes. At level 05, {{a_fat20}} % of fatal predator or trap hits come when food energy is below 20, against {{a_all20}} % of all steps outside a bush (Figure 3). That is consistent with hunger pushing agents out when it is risky, though the figure cannot show cause.

Each extra predator is costly. At level 05, episodes with no predator survive {{a_cnt0}} % of the time, with one predator {{a_cnt1}} %, with two {{a_cnt2}} % (Figure 4).

Which predators kill? In level-05 episodes with exactly one predator, survival falls steeply with the predator's sight (Figure 5). A predator that notices the agent from 3 cells and pounces from 3 — the setting of today's lookalike world — leaves {{a_s33}} % of episodes alive; with a pounce reach of 2, {{a_s32}} %. A pounce only fires during a chase, and a chase ends once the agent is more than 1.5 × the predator's sight away (core.py, _hunt_step), so at sight 1 the pounce reach makes no difference ({{a_s1}} % survive either way).

04 How each world enters the planner

The planner reuses the internal-state interaction study's model of a reduced level 05: five places (open cold ground, a cold bush, a bush on a fire ring, an open fire ring, and a food item), trips of fixed length between them, two maps (with and without a bush beside a fire, 41 % / 59 % of episodes), and the exact body rules. Its body update was checked against the environment's own code on {{vb_sets}} settings, including every candidate world's config as loaded and food energy 16 per bite, {{vb_n}} random states each (largest difference {{vb_max}}, no disagreement on deaths). The new hazard code reproduces the old planner's values exactly when fed the old study's measured hit odds (largest value difference {{vp_v}}), and the rebuilt level 05 matches the old study's result (survival {{v3_new}} % vs {{v3_old}} %; hide best in {{v3_newhide}} % vs {{v3_oldhide}} % of start states, on the map without a warm bush). The five-place model itself and its fixed trips are not validated against the real map.

  • Predator. The planner sees a chance of being hit, sized like a real predator hit (15–120 injury), on every step outside a bush. Its level-05 value comes from the old study's recordings of trained agents: hits of 60 or more can only be predators, giving {{p_pred_move}} predator hits per moving step. Each world scales that by how deadly its predators are relative to level 05 (Figure 13, left), times the expected number of predators. Sight 3 with pounce 3 is a deadly combination ({{lam33}} injury deaths per 1,000 steps outside a bush, against {{lam05}} for level 05's mix; the ratio is taken after the trap part is removed), so the lookalike setting gives {{scaleAm}} × level 05's hit odds; the new setting, sight and pounce 2–3 with exactly one predator, averages {{lamAp}} and gives {{scaleAp}} ×. A+ therefore means lower model hazard, as faced by today's trained agents with their evasion skill.
  • Food. Bites per item and energy per bite are the configs'. The trip to the next item is the median distance to the nearest of 1–4 items ({{trip14}} steps, the old study's measured value) or 1–3 items ({{trip13}} steps), on a 10 × 10 grid. After each bite the item moves with probability one over its bites, and the trip is paid again.
  • Traps and rocks. When visible, the trained agents' own measured hit rates, scaled by the expected count ({{trapvis}} trap hits per 100 travel steps in world 1). When invisible, the planner assumes the agent cannot route around them: a hit on a travel step has probability equal to their density, count / 100 cells ({{trapinv_min}}–{{trapinv_max}} trap hits per 100 travel steps). This is an assumption, probably too high: a remembering agent would avoid a trap after the first hit.

05 The pre-registered verdict

Before any world was solved, the rule was written down (docs/experiments/active/rebalance_planner/PREREGISTRATION.md): a world offers a real choice if hiding is the best move in at least 20 % of start states and eating is the best move in at least 20 %. What that bar really asks: eating is best in about 0 % of start states whose food energy is above 100, so 20 % of all start states means about {{bar_hungry}} % of the hungry ones.

No world meets it. Hiding is best in {{hide_min}}–{{hide_max}} % of start states and eating in {{eat_min}}–{{eat_max}} % (Figure 6; level 05: {{l05_hide}} % and {{l05_eat}} %). The closest is world 4 (the new predator setting, fewer and bigger food items) at {{w4_eat}} % ({{w4t4_eat}} % with a food trip of 4 instead of 5). Only {{nt_min}}–{{nt_max}} % of start states have hide and eat within 1 reward unit of each other (table below Figure 6).

12 Limits and disclosures

  • The forage share is capped near 50 % by construction. Eating is never best above the setpoint, which is about half of all start states.
  • Invisible traps are probably overstated. The planner assumes a hit on a travel step with probability equal to trap density; a real agent repeats paths and can remember where it was hit.
  • Trained agents' evasion is baked into the predator hazard. Survival numbers and the group A effect depend on it; the decision shares depend on it much less (halving or doubling the predator odds moves them by a few points, section 09).
  • The five-place model is a simplification. Fixed trips, no predator position, no searching; only the body update was validated against the environment, not the reduced map.
  • The objective is the environment's reward with discount 0.95, not survival as such; a policy that maximised survival steps could choose differently.
  • Disclosures. The pre-registration (docs/experiments/active/rebalance_planner/PREREGISTRATION.md) said 2,000 rollouts per map; the code ran 4,000. The change of group A to exactly one predator came after the first results were read; the verdict did not change. The hungry-states view, the lever sweeps (world 4 added at the verdict review) and the discount-0.99 check were added after the first results and are labelled exploratory or sensitivity.

13 What this means

With bush healing as it is, hiding is cheap: a typical predator hit heals in a dozen or so rest steps in a bush, and the bush is the only place no hazard reaches. So in the planner, hunger and injury rarely compete. When injured, the agent heals first and eats afterwards, and it has time to do both. The four groups of settings shift the balance by a few points, and fewer, bigger food items shift it the most. None makes eating and hiding comparably good across many body states. That might need a link between injury and hunger: for example, healing that costs food, or food that runs short while the agent hides. This is a hypothesis for a next study, not a conclusion of this one. It also leaves open what trained agents do when a predator is actually near, which only the planned training runs can show.
