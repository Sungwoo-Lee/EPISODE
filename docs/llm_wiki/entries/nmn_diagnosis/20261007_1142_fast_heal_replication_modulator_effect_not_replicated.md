# Fast-heal replication: the modulator's larger injury effect does not replicate

## Key conclusion
Across nine new ordinary/modulated pairs trained with fast bush healing (levels 04, 05, 06; seeds 42-44), the
modulated agent's injury effect on bush dwell is the larger one in only 4 of 9 pairs (level 04: 2/3; 05: 1/3;
06: 1/3), with a mean difference of about 1 percentage point. Both agent types learn injury-driven hiding; the
22-Sep gap came mostly from that run's ordinary agent being unusually low (+2 pp). A claim about the modulator
needs several seeds, because seed-to-seed variation is as large as the agent difference.

## Evidence, measurements, facts
- Injury effect (injury 70 minus 0, bush dwell, mean over the 0.2 M grid from 2 to 10 M steps), 9 replication pairs:
  no animal, ordinary mean +7 vs modulated +8; wandering rabbit +5 vs +5.
- Predator effect larger for the modulated agent in 6 of 9 pairs (about +5 pp mean) - suggestive only.
- Dangerous animals (predator, chasing rabbit) give 45-68 % dwell, flat across starting injury 0-90 for every agent:
  injury does not sharpen the response to danger.
- Level 04 seed 42 repeats the 22-Sep training exactly, yet its ordinary agent's injury effect is +11 vs +2 on
  22 Sep: training is not reproducible run for run.
- Neutral vs own test scenes in these agents: level 05 modulated larger 1/3 neutral vs 3/3 own - the opposite of the
  cross-run page's neutral-scene advantage ([[20261006_0758_test_scene_confound_level05_modulator_gap]]).
