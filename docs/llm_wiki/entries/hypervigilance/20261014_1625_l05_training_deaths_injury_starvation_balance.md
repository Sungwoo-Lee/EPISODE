# Level-05 agents train at a balance point between injury death and starvation

## Key conclusion
Death is not rare in level-05 training: about 40% of episodes end because injury reaches the limit, and another quarter end in starvation. Ordinary and modulated agents look the same on this (within about 1 percentage point). The agents seem to settle where hiding more would starve them and foraging more would get them killed. Our short test scenes (101 steps, nobody dies) never show this trade-off. An earlier guess of mine, that "death may be rare in training", was wrong.

## Evidence, measurements, facts
- Source: WandB project sungwoolee/grid_world_pain. Runs are selected by display name (regex on rppo_healrep_l05_ and rppo_nmninp_l05_), 10 runs in total. History is pulled with samples=3000, then averaged over the last 20% and over the 10-30% window.
- Episode endings, last 20% of training: Term_Injury 0.39-0.42, Term_MaxSteps 0.31-0.33, Term_Starvation 0.25-0.27, Term_Thermal about 0.02. Mean episode length is about 250 steps (cap 500).
- Damage per episode: DamagePredator about 75 and DamageDanger (hidden hazard) about 39, which is about 1.3 hidden-predator hits of about 30 points each. TotalDamage is about 125.
- Change from early (10-30%) to late training: injury deaths 0.43 to 0.39, step-cap endings 0.27 to 0.32, starvation flat.
- Tooling gotcha: the repo's `wandb_metrics.py timeseries` matched run folders to the wrong WandB runs (it picked level-04 runs, and the same run for all three ordinary labels). I listed runs by display-name regex with a scratch script instead.

## Decisions and actions
- The findings were shared with the behaviour session ("Behavior: experiment measure"). The user put any level-05 rebalancing **on hold**, so the findings are kept on record only.

## Open questions and follow-ups
- Log the injury level at the fatal hit, to see whether agents die already badly injured or from one large hit.
- Test scenes that can end in death (longer, or starting near the injury limit) would expose the trade-off.
