# In level 05, injury does not make a threat more costly, so it does not produce hypervigilance

## Key conclusion
The original idea was that a badly hurt agent can be killed by one predator attack or one hidden-predator hit, so it should treat any animal as more dangerous. In the current world that pressure is weak. Injury changes nothing about how much a threat costs: the same damage, the same speed, the same predator targeting. The one thing injury does change is that bush healing (5 injury points per step in cover) becomes worth more. So injured agents learn to sit and heal, not to be more wary of animals.

## Evidence, measurements, facts
- The extra bush time at injury 70 vs 0 is about +9 to +14 pp for every agent. The slope is 0.17 pp per injury point, against 0.20 predicted from healing time alone.
- Healing-off probe (run by the behaviour session; results under the `*_noheal` avoidance folders): injured agents still hide 76-96% at injury 90. No agent shows an injury x rabbit interaction once the ceiling is accounted for, and the modulated agents' rabbit gap is flat or negative above injury 20.
- Hidden predators cannot be detected, but the agent could still learn to anticipate them. That would need injury to raise the expected cost of a hit, and it doesn't.
- The training death balance is in [[20261014_1625_l05_training_deaths_injury_starvation_balance]].

## Decisions and actions
- Bush healing in training stays unchanged (user decision, 2026-10-11). Healing-off is a test-time probe only.
- Rebalancing ideas recorded, none planned: food placed near cover; an earlier predator warning; cover that hides without healing plus a healing spot that does not hide; injury that raises a threat's cost (more damage, slower movement, predators targeting injured prey).
- The user chose **"Hold for now"** on rebalancing and on the proposed work split with the behaviour session.

## Open questions and follow-ups
