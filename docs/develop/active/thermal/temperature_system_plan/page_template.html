Thermoregulation

A design for giving the agent a body temperature it can die of, a world with warm and cold places, and a sense that tells it which way is warmer.

PURPOSE

Today the agent has two things it must keep in range: how full it is, and how injured it is. This plan adds a third — body temperature — modelled on the temperature system in EVAAA, the Unity environment this project takes its interoceptive framing from.

The idea in one line: the world gets a temperature field, a number attached to every cell of the grid. Warm objects like rocks heat the cells around them; shaded ones like bushes cool them. The agent's own temperature drifts toward whatever the cell it is standing on is, so standing somewhere too hot or too cold eventually kills it, and it has a short-range sense of which neighbouring cells are warmer. Because temperature also enters the reward, the agent has to trade thermal comfort against food and safety rather than solving them one at a time.

This document is the plan, not the implementation. Nothing described here is built yet. It exists to be argued with — every number in it is a proposal, and the sections are ordered so you can reject one without unpicking the others.

01 The thermal field

The field is one number per grid cell, built once when the episode resets and then held fixed. It is built in four stages, in this order.

Random spots

Each spot picks a uniformly random cell, a sign, and paints a square of side spot_size around it at ±spot_temp. EVAAA paints only hot spots; allowing the sign to flip costs nothing and gives us cold refuges as well as hot ones, which matters because a world with only hot spots makes "move away from heat" a sufficient policy.

One departure from EVAAA: stamps add

EVAAA writes areaTemp[x,z] = obstacle.temperature — a straight assignment. Two overlapping sources therefore do not combine; the one spawned last silently wins. We propose adding instead. Two rocks beside each other make a hotter spot than one, which is the physically sensible reading, and the result no longer depends on spawn order — which matters for us in a way it does not for EVAAA, because our byte-parity tests would otherwise be at the mercy of iteration order.

Smoothing is what makes the field navigable

Stamping alone produces cliffs: a cell is either +20 or 0, and an agent standing next to one has no way to tell which direction leads somewhere warmer. The Gaussian blur turns those cliffs into slopes. It is not cosmetic — it is the step that makes the field something a gradient-following policy can act on at all.

Scale warning

EVAAA's shipped values are smoothingSigma: 3.0 with hotSpotSize: 20 on a 100×100 grid. Ours is 10×10. Copying those numbers across would blur the entire world into a single temperature and stamp spots larger than the map. Every constant in this document has been re-derived for our grid; none is inherited.

02 Temperature from objects

Any entity type may declare a thermal_output. At field-build time each active instance adds its value to the cell it occupies, before the blur. This is EVAAA's ObstacleTemperature component, read the same way our existing per-entity blocks_sight flag is read.

The appeal is that it gives scenery we already have a second job. Rocks become thermal mass and bushes become shade, so the same object that blocks sight also shapes the thermal landscape — and the agent's existing reason to sit in a bush (hiding from predators) now competes with a thermal reason to sit somewhere else.

Values are proposals. Setting every entity to 0 and relying on random spots alone is a valid configuration, and is the right control arm for measuring whether object-sourced heat matters.

03 Body temperature

The agent carries one number, body_temp, that relaxes toward the temperature of the cell it currently stands on. Each step:

# T = body temperature, T_field = temperature of the agent's own cell
T ← T + k_exchange·(T_field − T) + k_metabolic − k_loss·(T − T_neutral)

The first term is Newton's law of cooling: you drift toward your surroundings, faster when the gap is larger. The third pulls you back toward a neutral set point regardless of surroundings — a standing-in of physiological regulation. The middle term is constant heat production, zero by default.

Leaving the range [−15, +15] ends the episode, exactly as starvation does.

Why not EVAAA's formulation

EVAAA computes surroundTemp as the sum of its eight probe differentials and multiplies by one coefficient. Two consequences: the effective gain is eight times the coefficient, so changing the number of probes silently changes the body's time constant; and its change_3·(bodyTemp + 15) term is an unbounded exponential rather than a restoring force, so it has no set point to return to. The form above avoids both while producing the same qualitative behaviour.

04 What the thermoceptor reads

The agent samples the field over a small diamond of cells centred on itself and reports each one relative to its own body temperature — so the observation answers "which way is more comfortable than I am now", not "how hot is it here". At range 1 that is five numbers: the agent's cell and its four neighbours.

The one real choice here was the shape. EVAAA uses eight compass probes — a Moore neighbourhood, a 3×3 square. Every sensor we already have (olfaction, vision, collision) uses a Manhattan diamond, the von Neumann neighbourhood. So: does dropping the four corners cost anything?

Layoutcellserror at σ = 0.2verdict
von Neumann r = 15@@vn1@@°best accuracy, fewest dimensions — chosen
Moore r = 1 (EVAAA)9@@mo1@@°+4 dims for +0.4° of error
von Neumann r = 213@@vn2@@°wider is worse
Moore r = 225@@mo2@@°five times the cells, four times the error

Shape barely matters; radius matters about ten times more — and wider is worse. That is counter-intuitive until you notice that a plane fit assumes the field is locally flat. The field is a sum of Gaussians, so a wider stencil straddles more curvature and the fitted plane tilts away from the true tangent. That is a bias, not noise: it does not average out with more samples, which is why Moore r = 2 reads 25 cells and still loses to von Neumann r = 1 reading 5.

Extra cells only start paying once noise dominates bias — the crossover sits near σ = 0.4 on the left chart, and our configs currently run with perceptual noise switched off entirely.

Consequence for the config

Use the diamond at thermal_grid_range: 1, and match sensor radius to field smoothing — the right-hand chart shows radius 1 is the correct partner for σ ≈ 1.5. Do not add a Moore option: it buys under half a degree, costs four dimensions, and introduces a second neighbourhood geometry to test and maintain.

Method correction, kept on the record

An earlier version of this measurement used numpy.gradient as ground truth and reported von Neumann r = 1 as exactly perfect at zero noise. That was an artefact: numpy.gradient uses central differences, whose stencil is identical to a five-point plane fit, so the comparison was rigged in the diamond's favour. Every number here uses the analytic gradient of the Gaussian field instead. The conclusion survived; the margin shrank.

05 Rendering

The field is the only new pixel data, and it is a scalar per cell — so it belongs underneath everything else as a background layer, with entities drawn on top exactly as they are now. A diverging blue–red scale with a neutral midpoint reads correctly here because temperature has a meaningful zero: the set point the agent is trying to hold.

Three additions, in decreasing order of how much they are worth:

field underlay
The diverging scale, drawn beneath the grid lines. Fixed colour limits across a whole episode — per-frame rescaling would make a cooling world look stable.
body-temp gauge
A bar with the death thresholds marked, beside the existing satiation and injury readouts. Without it a video shows the world's temperature but not the agent's.
read-cell outline
Debug-only. Cheap to draw, and it makes an off-by-one in the sampling stencil visible instead of silent.

06 Reward

The drive is the distance of the body's state from its set point. Today it has two axes; temperature makes three, each divided by its own range so that an equal fractional deviation pulls equally:

drive = ‖( (satiation − setpoint)/max_satiation ,
           injury/max_injury ,
           (T − T_neutral)/max_thermal_deviation )‖

Reward stays what it is now — the reduction in drive from one step to the next — so warming up when you are cold is rewarded and drifting toward an extreme is punished, without any new reward term.

This is the change with the widest blast radius in the whole plan, and it must be gated: with thermal.enabled: false the drive has to be bit-identical to today's, or every past run becomes incomparable.

07 Configuration

thermal:
  enabled: false            # gate; every key below is read only when true

  # --- field (section 01) ---
  default_temp: 0.0
  sigma: 1.5                # > 0 enforced at load
  random_spots:
    count: 2
    size: 2
    temp: 20.0              # sign chosen per spot
  use_object_sources: true  # section 02

  # --- body (section 03) ---
  neutral_temp: 0.0
  death_low: -15.0          # low < high enforced at load
  death_high: 15.0
  k_exchange: 0.04
  k_loss: 0.01
  k_metabolic: 0.0
  metabolic_coupling: false # when true, defending T draws on nutrition

  # --- sensor (section 04) ---
  grid_range: 1
  relative: true            # report field − body_temp

# per entity, alongside the existing blocks_sight flag:
obstacles:
  - name: rock
    blocks_sight: true
    thermal_output: 18.0

Sub-keys are conditional-mandatory: read only when enabled is true, and validated at load when they are read. That is the pattern the blur and occlusion knobs settled on, and it exists because an unvalidated sigma_floor: 0.0 once produced an all-NaN observation that trained silently.

08 Implementation order

Each stage is independently testable. Stages 0 to 2 must leave every existing config byte-identical — if they do not, something is wired in that should be gated.

STAGE 0
Parity harness, before any change
Snapshot observation, reward and drive for a sample of configs on a pinned CPU backend. This is the gate every later stage runs against. Building it first is what caught the sigma-floor and mask-leak bugs during the sensor work.
STAGE 1
The field
thermal_field in state, built at reset: fill, spots, object stamps, blur. No observation change, no body change. Parity must hold.
STAGE 2
Body temperature
body_temp and its update; death adds a sixth termination reason. Not observable and not in the drive yet.
STAGE 3
The thermoceptor
Diamond sampling, reusing the olfactory grid machinery with a scalar field. Observation width grows by five. This is the stage that breaks curriculum compatibility.
STAGE 4
Reward
The third drive axis, gated so that with thermal off the returned value is bit-identical to today's.
STAGE 5
Metabolic coupling
Off by default. When on, defending body temperature draws on nutrition.
STAGE 6
Rendering and documentation
Field underlay, body-temp gauge, read-cell debug outline; then the config guide, schema and sensor docs that the maintenance contract requires.

09 Known hazards

observation width
Five new dimensions change obs_dim, which the curriculum-stage compatibility check tests first. Any curriculum spanning thermal and non-thermal stages will be rejected. This is the same trap the olfactory-range change hit.
reward comparability
Once temperature enters the drive, reward is incomparable with every previous run. Gating is mandatory, not a nicety.
load-time validation
sigma > 0 and death_low < death_high. Skipping this is how the sensor work produced an all-NaN observation that trained without complaint.
termination codes
Reasons currently run 0–4; thermal death needs 5, and every consumer that enumerates them needs updating in the same change.
inherited constants
None of EVAAA's numbers transfer — different grid size, different clock. Every constant here was re-derived; treat any future one the same way.

10 Open questions

These are the places where the plan is genuinely undecided, and where feedback changes what gets built.

Should creatures carry heat?
A predator that is warm becomes detectable through the thermal sense at range, which is either a rich second detection channel or an accidental radar that trivialises predator avoidance. Currently proposed at 0.
Is a static field enough?
A field fixed for the whole episode can be memorised. The agent may learn the map rather than the sense. A day/night offset would break that, and the hook is left in place, but it is not in this plan.
Should the death range be symmetric?
±15 treats overheating and freezing as equally fast. Real thermoregulation is not symmetric, and asymmetry would make warm and cold refuges behaviourally distinct rather than mirror images.
Does the agent need to know its own temperature?
The thermoceptor reports the field relative to body temperature, so the agent can tell which way is warmer without knowing how hot it is. Injury and nutrition are both currently hidden (injury_observable: false), so hiding temperature too is the consistent choice — but it means the agent must infer its own state from the consequences.
One field per episode, or one per world?
Re-randomising the field every episode forces the sense to be used. Fixing it across an experiment makes runs more comparable. Proposed: re-randomise, matching how entities already spawn.
