Thermoregulation

A cold world, a fire worth returning to, and a body that will die if the agent forgets about either.

PURPOSE

Today the agent has two things it must keep in range: how full it is, and how injured it is. This plan adds a third — body temperature — modelled on the temperature system in EVAAA, the Unity environment this project takes its interoceptive framing from.

The shape of it: the world is cold everywhere, and the only warmth comes from a campfire. The agent's temperature drifts toward whatever cell it is standing on, so away from the fire it cools steadily and eventually dies; near the fire it recovers. Food grows away from the fire, so every meal is a trip out into the cold and back. Because temperature also enters the reward, the agent cannot solve hunger and warmth one at a time — it has to trade them against each other.

This document is the plan, not the implementation. Nothing here is built. Every number is a proposal, and the sections are ordered so you can reject one without unpicking the others.

01 Two ways to build the field

The field is one number per grid cell, built once at episode reset and then held fixed. Four stages, in order: fill every cell with default_temp, add any random spots, add each object's temperature to the cell it occupies, then blur the whole thing once.

The two middle stages are the two modes, and they are independent config flags — exactly as EVAAA has useRandomHotSpot and useObjectHotSpot. Either, or both, or neither. They are not variations on a theme: they produce opposite worlds and ask the agent for different competences.

Random-field mode

Each spot picks a uniformly random cell, a sign, and paints a square of side spot_size at ±spot_temp. Allowing the sign to flip is a departure from EVAAA, which paints only hot spots; it costs nothing and it stops "move away from heat" being a sufficient policy.

Amplitude is the difficulty dial and it is sharp: at temp = 20 the map is @@r20@@% survivable and the hazards are decorative; at 40 it is @@r40@@%, a real avoidance task; at 120 it is @@r120@@% and half the world will kill you. Proposed default 40. The escape distance stays around one cell throughout, so this mode never demands planning — one correct step is always enough.

Object mode: a cold world and a fire

Here default_temp goes negative and objects carry the heat. This is what EVAAA's own thermal experiments do: exp-two-resource-temp sets fieldDefaultTemp: -20.0 with hotSpotCount: 0 and a single campfire at 20,000; exp-riskTaking uses three fires at 10,000 each, every other object left at zero.

This is the mode that creates structure. A world that is safe by default gives the agent nothing to organise its behaviour around; a world that is hostile by default with one warm place in it creates a destination, and turns foraging into round trips against a clock (§3).

Both at once

Nothing prevents it, and EVAAA allows it. A cold world with a campfire and scattered patches gives a refuge plus a hazardous route to it — probably the most interesting version, and the one I would reach for third, after each mode has been characterised alone.

02 None of these are fixed numbers

Everything above is written as though the world had one temperature and one fire. It should not. The environment already samples nearly every entity property per episode — count_low/count_high for how many spawn, damage: [1, 5], attack_range: [2, 3], detection_range: [1, 7] — and the thermal system should follow the same convention rather than inventing a fixed-constant one.

So the campfire count, each fire's temperature, and the world's baseline are all ranges, drawn afresh at every reset. There is no optimal set to find; there is a distribution to choose. This also removes a hazard I flagged earlier — with a fixed field the agent can memorise the map instead of using the sense — because no two episodes give it the same world to memorise.

Candidate combinations

Four ranges worth considering, scored on how much of the map ends up survivable — the 10th to 90th percentile across 600 sampled episodes:

@@combo_rows@@
Combinationfirestemperature world baseline% safe (p10–p90) medianstructure holdscharacter

The column to read is structure holds — the share of sampled episodes that still deliver pain on the fire, safety beside it, and lethal cold beyond. A range scoring below 100% quietly produces episodes with no fire pain, or with no survivable ring, and those episodes train the agent on a different task than the one you specified. A, B and C hold everywhere in their box; D does not, which is the price of its width.

Among the three that hold, the choice is how much episode-to-episode variety you want. B is proposed: three possible fire counts and a six-degree spread of world baselines, so the map cannot be memorised, while every draw is still a real thermal problem.

One constraint applied throughout: fires spawn at least two cells from the edge. Without it a fire in the corner wastes most of its warm zone off-map, and episode difficulty swings for a reason unrelated to the range you chose.

Stamps add, they do not overwrite

EVAAA writes areaTemp[x,z] = obstacle.temperature — a straight assignment, so two overlapping sources do not combine and the one spawned last silently wins. We propose adding. Two fires beside each other make a hotter spot than one, which is the physically sensible reading, and the result stops depending on spawn order — which matters for us in a way it does not for EVAAA, because our byte-parity tests would otherwise be at the mercy of iteration order.

Smoothing is what makes the fire reachable

Stamping alone produces a cliff: one cell is hot and its neighbour is not, so an agent standing beside the fire has no gradient telling it which way to step. The blur turns the cliff into a slope the agent can climb. It is not cosmetic — without it the thermal sense has nothing to report until the agent is already standing on the fire.

Scale warning

EVAAA's shipped values — smoothingSigma: 5.0, campfire temperature 20000 — are for a 100×100 grid. Ours is 10×10. Copying them across would blur the world into one temperature and set the map on fire. Every constant here was re-derived; none is inherited.

03 Every object can carry a temperature

Each entity type declares a temperature, defaulting to 0.0. At build time every active instance adds its value to the cell it occupies, before the blur. This mirrors EVAAA exactly: every obstacle group in its configs carries a temperature field, and nearly all of them sit at 0.0.

The point of making it universal is that we do not have to decide now. If we later want cold bushes as shade, or warm rocks that hold the day's heat, or food that must be eaten near the fire, the mechanism is already there and it is a config change rather than a code change.

The campfire, not warm rocks

An earlier draft of this plan made rocks the heat source. That was wrong on the physics — a rock in a cold world is a cold rock — and it is not what EVAAA does. EVAAA ships a dedicated SM_campfire_001_AmbienceFX prefab and gives it the entire thermal budget: 20,000 in the two-resource temperature task, and three fires at 10,000 each in the risk-taking task, with every other object left at zero.

So we add a campfire object whose job is to be the heat source, and leave the existing scenery thermally neutral until there is a reason not to.

Every value here is a config default, not a constant in the code. Setting the campfire to zero and turning on random spots instead is a valid configuration, and is the right control arm for asking whether a localised heat source matters at all.

04 The thermal tether

Put a cold default and one fire together and you get the behaviour the whole system exists to produce: a leash. The agent can go as far as it likes, but the clock starts as soon as it leaves the warm zone, and it has to be back before the clock runs out. How long that leash is, is the single most consequential number in this plan.

The fire has to hurt

A fire you can sit on indefinitely is a radiator, not a fire. What we want is what a real animal does: creep close enough to be warm, and no closer. That means the cell the fire occupies should be painful — and painful without adding a nociceptor or a second internal state, purely by letting the temperature dynamics overshoot.

And it has to hurt fast. An earlier version of this design killed in 16 steps on the fire, which is attrition, not pain — for comparison a predator lands 15 to 120 damage in a single step. Retuned so that walking onto the fire from the comfort ring kills in @@burn@@ steps, which puts it in the same register as the other acute hazards.

This is achievable with a single Gaussian, but only in a narrow band. The fire's own cell must drive the body clean past the heat-death threshold while the cell next to it settles somewhere liveable. Searched over smoothing and fire strength, the shape appears at σ = @@sigma@@ with a campfire near @@afire@@ in a world at @@default@@ — a fire-cell ambient of +@@peak@@:

d = 0  on the fire
ambient +@@peak@@ drives the body to @@pain_eq@@, far past the +15 threshold — dead @@burn@@ steps after stepping on. One step is a sharp, recoverable jolt; three is fatal.
d = 1  beside it
settles at @@comfy_eq@@, the closest any cell comes to the set point — so this ring is both the safest place and the best-rewarded one.
d = 2
@@cool_eq@@ — cool, uncomfortable, survivable indefinitely.
d = 3 and beyond
lethal cold, @@cold_steps@@ steps and falling.

The behavioural consequence is the one you asked for: the agent should learn to orbit the fire rather than occupy it. Both directions off the comfort ring are punished, by different mechanisms — heat inward, cold outward — and the reward gradient does the fine work in between, since d = 1 is simply the cell whose equilibrium sits nearest the set point.

One consequence worth being explicit about: the same sensor reports both dangers. The thermoceptor returns field − body temperature, so nothing in the observation itself distinguishes "too hot" from "too cold" — the agent has to learn the sign convention from consequences, exactly as it does with nociception today.

Why this is a narrow band

Making the fire hurt quickly means concentrating it, and concentrating it costs the approach gradient. At σ = 0.5 the fire kills in 2 steps but the field jumps from +222 on the fire to +8 beside it to −23 two cells out and then flat — a cliff, with no slope to climb from any distance. Smoother and the peak flattens until the fire stops hurting at all: at σ = 1.2 the same campfire leaves its own cell merely warm.

σ = @@sigma@@ was chosen because it is the point where both survive. It kills in @@burn@@ steps and leaves a measurable slope out to three cells — four times the gradient of the σ = 0.5 version at the same burn time.

Fire strength is tighter still. Below about 290 the fire takes more than four steps to kill; above about 370 the comfort ring itself becomes lethal and the agent has nowhere to stand.

The range has to scale with the world

This is the part that bites when the values are sampled rather than fixed. The valid fire strength moves with the world's baseline: in a −22 world the window is roughly 265–315, in a −28 world it is 305–365. Sampling both independently over the ranges in §2 leaves an overlap only ten units wide, and draws outside it silently produce either a fire that does not hurt or a ring that is not survivable.

Two ways out, both measured over 400 sampled episodes:

narrow the absolute range
temperature: [275, 315] against default_temp: [-28, -22] holds the structure in 100% of draws — but the fire barely varies.
scale it to the world  (recommended)
Sample the fire as a multiple of the world's coldness — 11–13 × |default_temp|. That holds in 100% of draws over the same world range, and still 94% over a much wider [-32, -20], because a colder world automatically gets a stronger fire.

The second is the better design and it is barely more config: one ratio instead of one temperature. It also expresses something true — a fire that is a refuge in a mild winter is not a refuge in a hard one.

05 Body temperature

The agent carries one number, body_temp, that relaxes toward the temperature of the cell it stands on. Each step:

# T = body temperature, T_field = temperature of the agent's own cell
T ← T + k_exchange·(T_field − T) + k_metabolic − k_loss·(T − temperature_setpoint)

The first term is Newton's law of cooling: you drift toward your surroundings, faster when the gap is larger. The third pulls you back toward a neutral set point regardless of surroundings — a stand-in for physiological regulation, and the reason the agent does not simply equalise with the world. The middle term is constant heat production, zero by default.

Leaving [−15, +15] ends the episode, exactly as starvation does.

Reading the equation: a tug of war

The equation has two restoring terms, and it is easy to miss that they pull in different directions. k_exchange drags the body toward the cell it is standing on. k_loss drags it back toward its own set point, whatever the cell is doing. The body ends up somewhere between the two, never at either.

k_loss is the agent's physiology — shivering, vasoconstriction, the metabolic work of defending a body temperature. It is the term that makes the agent an animal rather than a thermometer.

Setting the step change to zero gives the equilibrium directly — a weighted average of the two attractors:

T* = ( k_exchange·T_field  +  k_loss·setpoint ) / ( k_exchange + k_loss )

So the body adopts a fraction of whatever the cell is offering, and that fraction is fixed by the ratio of the two constants:

adopted fraction
k_exchange / (k_exchange + k_loss) = @@adopted@@% at the proposed values. Stand in a −25 cell and the body settles at −20, not −25 — @@held@@% of the cold is held off by physiology.
survivable ambient
±15 · (k_exchange + k_loss) / k_exchange = ±@@window@@. Any cell colder than that is eventually fatal; anything inside it can be endured forever. This single number decides the safe radius around the fire.
time constant
1 / (k_exchange + k_loss) = @@tau@@ steps. Note k_loss appears here too, so raising it makes the body both more resistant and faster to respond.

The two ways to get this wrong

Set k_loss to zero and the body becomes a passive thermometer. It adopts the cell's temperature exactly, the set point stops meaning anything, and every cell outside [−15, +15] is fatal with no exceptions — there is no band of cold-but-survivable ground, so the field becomes a binary map of safe and deadly cells with no gradient of discomfort in between.

Set it too high and physiology wins outright. At k_loss = @@kceil@@ the survivable window reaches ±25 and our default_temp of @@default@@ can no longer kill; every cell in the world becomes survivable and the thermal task quietly disappears while still appearing to be configured. This is a hard ceiling, and it is the one number in this plan most likely to be changed by accident.

The proposed @@kloss@@ sits well below it: the body defends partially, which is what makes cold uncomfortable long before it is lethal, and therefore what makes the reward gradient do useful work in the region where nothing is dying yet.

Where the cost lives

Right now this defence is free. The agent resists the cold at no charge, which is exactly what k_metabolic and the metabolic_coupling flag exist to change: turning coupling on makes the work represented by k_loss draw on nutrition, so staying warm competes with staying fed. That is the single most interesting extension in this plan, and it is deliberately off by default so the thermal system can be characterised on its own first.

Why not EVAAA's formulation

EVAAA computes surroundTemp as the sum of its eight probe differentials and multiplies by one coefficient. Two consequences: the effective gain is eight times that coefficient, so changing the probe count silently changes the body's time constant; and its change_3·(bodyTemp + 15) term is an unbounded exponential with no set point to return to. The form above avoids both and behaves the same qualitatively.

tau = @@tau@@ steps
One time constant, 1/(k_exchange + k_loss). Long enough that a single misstep is survivable, short enough that a bad habit compounds within an episode.
k_exchange > @@kcrit@@
Below this the far field can never kill and temperature stops being a survival pressure at all. Proposed value is 0.04, comfortably above it.

06 What the thermoceptor reads

The agent samples the field over a small diamond centred on itself and reports each cell relative to its own body temperature — so the observation answers "which way is warmer than I am", not "how hot is it here". At range 1 that is five numbers: its own cell and its four neighbours.

The one real choice was the shape. EVAAA uses eight compass probes — a Moore neighbourhood, a 3×3 square. Every sensor we already have uses a Manhattan diamond, the von Neumann neighbourhood. Does dropping the four corners cost anything?

Layoutcellserror at σ = 0.2verdict
von Neumann r = 15@@vn1@@°best accuracy, fewest dimensions — chosen
Moore r = 1 (EVAAA)9@@mo1@@°+4 dims for +0.4° of error
von Neumann r = 213@@vn2@@°wider is worse
Moore r = 225@@mo2@@°five times the cells, four times the error

Shape barely matters; radius matters about ten times more — and wider is worse. That is counter-intuitive until you notice a plane fit assumes the field is locally flat. It is not: a wider stencil straddles more curvature, so the fitted plane tilts away from the true tangent. That is a bias, not noise — it does not average out with more samples, which is why Moore r = 2 reads 25 cells and still loses to von Neumann r = 1 reading 5.

Extra cells only pay once noise dominates bias — the crossover sits near σ = 0.4, and our configs currently run with perceptual noise off entirely.

Consequence for the config

Use the diamond at thermal_grid_range: 1. Do not add a Moore option: it buys under half a degree, costs four dimensions, and introduces a second neighbourhood geometry to test and maintain.

Method correction, kept on the record

An earlier version of this measurement used numpy.gradient as ground truth and reported von Neumann r = 1 as exactly perfect at zero noise. That was an artefact: numpy.gradient uses central differences, whose stencil is identical to a five-point plane fit, so the comparison was rigged in the diamond's favour. Every number here uses the analytic gradient of the Gaussian field instead. The conclusion survived; the margin shrank.

07 Rendering

The field is the only new pixel data and it is a scalar per cell, so it belongs underneath everything else, with entities drawn on top exactly as they are now. A diverging blue–red scale reads correctly here because temperature has a meaningful zero: the set point the agent is trying to hold.

field underlay
The diverging scale beneath the grid lines, with colour limits fixed for the whole episode — per-frame rescaling would make a cooling world look stable.
body-temp gauge
A bar with the death thresholds marked, beside the existing satiation and injury readouts. Without it a video shows the world's temperature but not the agent's, which is the half that actually matters.
read-cell outline
Debug-only. Cheap to draw, and it makes an off-by-one in the sampling stencil visible instead of silent.

08 Reward

The drive is the distance of the body's state from its set point. Today it has two axes; temperature makes three, each divided by its own range so an equal fractional deviation pulls equally:

drive = ‖( (satiation − setpoint)/max_satiation ,
           injury/max_injury ,
           (T − temperature_setpoint)/max_thermal_deviation )‖

Reward stays what it is now — the reduction in drive from one step to the next — so moving toward the fire while cold is rewarded and drifting outward is punished, with no new reward term.

This is the change with the widest blast radius in the plan, and it must be gated: with thermal.enabled: false the drive has to be bit-identical to today's, or every past run becomes incomparable.

Sub-keys are conditional-mandatory: read only when enabled is true, and validated at load when they are read. That is the pattern the blur and occlusion knobs settled on, and it exists because an unvalidated sigma_floor: 0.0 once produced an all-NaN observation that trained silently.

10 Implementation order

Each stage is independently testable. Stages 0 to 2 must leave every existing config byte-identical — if they do not, something is wired in that should be gated.

STAGE 0
Parity harness, before any change
Snapshot observation, reward and drive for a sample of configs on a pinned CPU backend. Every later stage runs against it. Building this first is what caught the sigma-floor and mask-leak bugs during the sensor work.
STAGE 1
The field and the campfire object
thermal_field in state, built at reset; temperature read on every entity type; the new campfire obstacle. No observation change, no body change. Parity must hold.
STAGE 2
Body temperature
body_temp and its update; death adds a sixth termination reason. Not observable and not in the drive yet.
STAGE 3
The thermoceptor
Diamond sampling, reusing the olfactory grid machinery with a scalar field. Observation width grows by five — this is the stage that breaks curriculum compatibility.
STAGE 4
Reward
The third drive axis, gated so that with thermal off the returned value is bit-identical to today's.
STAGE 5
Metabolic coupling
Off by default. When on, defending body temperature draws on nutrition.
STAGE 6
Rendering and documentation
Field underlay, body-temp gauge, read-cell debug outline; then the config guide, schema and sensor docs the maintenance contract requires.

11 Known hazards

observation width
Five new dimensions change obs_dim, which the curriculum-stage compatibility check tests first. Any curriculum spanning thermal and non-thermal stages will be rejected. The same trap the olfactory-range change hit.
reward comparability
Once temperature enters the drive, reward is incomparable with every previous run. Gating is mandatory, not a nicety.
load-time validation
sigma > 0 and min_temperature < max_temperature. Skipping this is how the sensor work produced an all-NaN observation that trained without complaint.
termination codes
Reasons currently run 0–4; thermal death needs 5, and every consumer that enumerates them needs updating in the same change.
campfire placement
If the fire can spawn in a corner, the reachable warm zone shrinks and the task changes difficulty between episodes for a reason unrelated to the sense. Worth pinning to the centre or constraining the spawn area.
inherited constants
None of EVAAA's numbers transfer — different grid, different clock. Every constant here was re-derived; treat any future one the same way.

12 Open questions

Where the plan is genuinely undecided, and where your feedback changes what gets built.

Is the pain window too narrow to be safe?
The pain-plus-comfort structure exists only for σ near @@sigma@@ and a fire within roughly 11–13× the world's coldness. A config drifting outside it silently loses either the fire pain or the comfort ring, and the run still looks healthy. This deserves a load-time check that actually simulates the profile, not just a documented range — it is cheap to compute once at reset and it is the difference between a failed experiment and a noticed one.
One fire or several?
EVAAA's risk-taking task uses three. Multiple fires turn a leash into a network and let food be placed between refuges, which is a much more interesting foraging problem than radial trips.
Should creatures carry heat?
A warm predator becomes detectable through the thermal sense at range, which is either a rich second detection channel or an accidental radar that trivialises predator avoidance. Currently proposed at 0.
Should the death range be symmetric?
±15 treats overheating and freezing as equally fast. In a cold-by-default world the cold threshold does nearly all the work, so the two are not symmetric in practice even though they are in the config.
Does the agent need to know its own temperature?
The thermoceptor reports the field relative to body temperature, so the agent can tell which way is warmer without knowing how cold it is. Injury and nutrition are both currently hidden, so hiding temperature is the consistent choice — but it means the agent must infer how close to death it is entirely from consequences.
Should food spawn away from the fire?
The tether only creates a trade-off if food is out in the cold. If food can spawn next to the fire, the agent may never need to leave, and the whole system collapses into a stationary policy.
