Reading Anthropic's Figure Style

A working transcription of the design system behind transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace — taken from its three stylesheets rather than from looking at it — and what it would take to put this project's own figures into that style. This page is rendered in the system it describes, so it doubles as the specimen.

How this was obtained, and what that limits

The page links three stylesheets. All three were fetched and read: public/paper.css (24 KB), public/shared-styles.css (21 KB) and public/bundle.css (147 KB). Every value in this document is quoted from them.

That matters because the first attempt at this used a summarising fetch, which returned a plausible and largely wrong answer: it reported the body text as 16px (it is 13px), the typefaces as a generic system stack (they are three proprietary Anthropic faces with system fallbacks), and the figure palette as “viridis or similar” (the two series colours are Paul Tol's blue and orange). A style document built on that would have been confidently wrong in exactly the details it exists to get right.

One finding that changes how to read every colour below

In the source, every grey is written as a fallback pair — --text: var(--gray-700, #333), --tick-line: var(--gray-300, #ddd), and so on. None of the --gray-N tokens is defined in any of the three stylesheets. So the fallback is what renders: #333 really is the body ink, #ddd really is the tick colour. The greys in this document are the rendered values, not the intended ones.

The same check finds two tokens that are used but never defined — --brand-manilla and --brand-clay, both consumed by rich-text annotation colours. Those declarations resolve to nothing in the sheets fetched here; they are presumably set by the page's template script, which is not a stylesheet and was not read.

Type

Three faces, one role each, all with system fallbacks. The proprietary faces will not load outside Anthropic's own pages — including on this page, which is therefore rendering in the fallbacks. That is the honest state of things rather than a defect to hide: the scale and the hierarchy transfer, the letterforms do not.

--font-sans · 'Anthropic Sans', system-ui, …

Section heading — 26px / 600

Subsection — 19px / 600 / 1.4

Body text at 13px with a 1.6 line-height. Small for a web page and deliberately so: it is an academic measure, set to put a lot of argument in front of the reader without making the column wide.

--font-mono · 'Anthropic Mono', ui-monospace, …

Mono carries labels, legends and figure furniture — not just code. legend-item sets font-family: var(--font-mono).

--font-serif · 'Anthropic Serif', Georgia, …

Serif is declared but barely used — two rules in 190 KB of CSS. It is not the body face.

Colour

The palette is almost entirely achromatic. Colour is spent on data and on one accent, and nowhere else — there is no coloured chrome, no tinted headings, no branded rules.

The two series colours are a named, colourblind-safe pair

#0077bb and #ee7733 are the blue and orange of Paul Tol's qualitative scheme, a palette designed so that its members stay distinguishable under the common forms of colour vision deficiency. The source names them as such: --series-blue: var(--tol-blue, #0077bb). Two facts follow. Blue and orange are chosen *because* they survive; and the scheme has more members, so a third series has a defined next colour rather than a free choice.

Layout

A named-line CSS grid in the Distill tradition: columns are named rather than numbered, so a figure declares how far it breaks out by naming an edge, not by computing a width.

10 columns × 45px, 16px gaps

text-start → text-end  (472px)
gutter
text-start → gutter-end
page-start → page-end

The text measure is eight 45px columns plus seven 16px gaps = 472px — computed from the grid declaration, not measured in a browser, so treat it as the specification rather than as a rendered fact. Figures step outward by naming text-end, gutter-end or screen-end; all three appear in the source. Captions carry their own max-width: 720px, so a caption is allowed to be wider than the text it explains.

Vertical rhythm is set once and left alone: figure { margin: 48px auto }, and figure + figure { margin-top: 64px } — two figures in a row get more air than a figure after prose, which is a detail most systems forget.

Figures — the part worth copying

This is where the system is most opinionated, and most of it is subtraction.

The five rules, in the order they matter

1. The axis spine is deleted outright. .axis path.domain { display: none }. Not lightened — removed. What remains of the axis is a set of faint horizontal rules in #ddd at the tick values, so the eye reads values off the chart rather than off a frame around it.

2. Axis text is 11px in #888; the axis title is 13px in #333 at weight 600. A two-step hierarchy inside the figure itself: the title of the quantity is darker and heavier than the numbers on the scale, so a reader scanning finds out what is plotted before how much.

3. A title above, a caption below. .fig-title is 15px/600 in #333 with 14px of space beneath it; the caption is 13px in #888, upright, not italic. Most systems have one or the other. Having both splits two jobs: the title says what you are looking at, the caption says what to conclude.

4. Legends are set in mono, and a line swatch is 14×3px. A line series gets a 14×3 rectangle — a segment of the line itself, not a filled square; a point series gets a 10px dot. Legends sit centred below the chart with a 16px gap, never floating inside the plot.

5. Two data colours, and they are chosen for vision, not taste. Everything else in the figure is grey.

What this style does not do, which is as instructive

No chart junk, obviously — but more specifically: no coloured backgrounds behind plots, no drop shadows, no rounded plot frames, no gradient fills, no legends boxed in a border, no italic captions, and no colour used to mean “good” or “bad”. The greys carry all structure; the two hues carry only which series is which.

Putting this project's figures into that style

Our figures are matplotlib PNGs, not D3. The conventions above translate almost directly, and the translation is short enough to live in one shared style module rather than being repeated per script. What follows is the mapping, not a claim that it has been applied yet.

legend below, centred, no frameax.legend(loc="upper center", bbox_to_anchor=(.5,-.12), ncol=2, frameon=False)
title above the figure, not on the axesemit as page markup, not ax.set_title

Two of our own rules this style would collide with

Legibility floor. An 11px tick label is fine at the size they display figures. Ours are rendered around 1500–2300px wide and shown in a 730px column, so an 11pt label lands near 5px — well under the 9px floor this project enforces. Adopting their sizes literally would break our figures; adopting their ratios (labels smaller than the axis title, axis title smaller than the figure title) is what transfers.

Colour meaning. Our pages already spend purple, blue and green on what a modulator reads. Their blue and orange would collide with that on any page that carries both. The resolution is theirs, in fact: greys carry structure, and hue is reserved for the one distinction the argument turns on — whichever distinction that happens to be on a given page.
