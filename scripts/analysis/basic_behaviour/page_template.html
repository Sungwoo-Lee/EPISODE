What the trained agents do, and which settings move it

A standard first look at a trained population: how much of its time each agent spends on five behaviours, how long it survives, and which features of the world and which training settings push each behaviour up or down. {{STATUS_LINE}}

PURPOSE

After training, every agent in a study is replayed for about a million evaluation episodes and every step is recorded. This page summarises those recordings the same way for every population, so that two studies can be read side by side. It measures five behaviours, each as a share of the steps the agent chose in an episode: bush dwell (time spent in a bush), eating, being within two squares of a rabbit, being within two squares of a predator, and, in worlds with temperature, standing on a warm square. It also reports survival, measured in steps, which is how this project judges performance.

The page then asks which features of each episode (how many predators, how injured the agent started, how predator-like a rabbit smells, how cold the world is) go with more or less of each behaviour, run by run; and, across runs, which settings — the smell world, the agent type, the training seed — move each behaviour most. That last part is exploratory screening to help choose a training direction. It is not a test of any study's pre-registered question, and it draws no conclusions: interpretation is written separately.

WATER What changes in worlds with a pond

Every world of this population has one pond per episode and a hydration level that drains a little every step and rises only while the agent stands on the pond. Running dry (died of thirst) and filling past the maximum (over-drank) end an episode, so Figure 1 shows these two ways an episode can end as well. The page measures a sixth behaviour, time on the pond (drinking): the share of chosen steps on which the agent stands on a pond cell. The pond's position is not stored with the recordings; it is rebuilt from each episode's random seed by replaying the environment's own reset, and every step of every episode is checked (hydration rose exactly when the agent stood on the pond) — {{W_CHECKS}}. Hydration at the start of an episode and the start distance to the pond are new episode features; the pond's corner is tabulated in Figure 2, not fitted. In Figure 6 the hydration panels for the pond count only steps where the agent was off the pond one step earlier, so they show how often a thirsty agent arrives, not how long a drinking bout lasts.

07 Experiment tests: bush dwell in controlled scenes

Exploratory · added 2026-10-02

This section is not part of the smell study's pre-registered test and cannot change its verdict. Its reading rules were written down before any experiment-test result existed, in the study document's section “Probe-scene sweep (exploratory)” (the document heading uses the earlier name for these experiment tests).

Figures 1 to 6 watch each agent in the world it trained in, where many things happen at once. Here the same 18 agents are put into twelve small, fixed experiment test scenes instead: a 10×10 arena with one hiding bush, a campfire beside it, and at most one animal. Six animal setups — no animal, a hunting predator, a harmless chasing rabbit, the chasing rabbit with its predator-like smell channel removed, a harmless wandering rabbit, and a wandering rabbit that smells like a predator — each run once with the agent unhurt (injury 0) and once badly injured (injury 70 of 100). Every one of the 50 checkpoints saved during training is replayed, 30 episodes per scene, and the measure is bush dwell: the share of the scene's steps the agent spends on the bush.

The last setup asks this study's question directly: does a harmless animal that merely smells like a predator make the agent dwell in the bush more (the confusion contrast), and does injury make that larger? Two things to keep in mind. The campfire next to the bush makes the bush the warm place in a cold arena, which lifts bush dwell even when nothing threatens; compare scenes with each other, not with the training environment. And in the single-channel world the “smell removed” chasing rabbit has no smell at all, because its one smell channel is the one removed, so that scene is not compared across worlds.

What these figures cannot tell you

Whether a difference between worlds would survive more training seeds: three per world is a screen. Nor whether the experiment-test readings agree with the study's pre-registered reading of the training environment; if the two disagree in sign, the pre-registered one stands.
